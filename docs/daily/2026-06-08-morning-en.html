📊 Key Facts: 50% 50% 30,000
Background

No-CoT reasoning means models solve complex tasks without producing explicit reasoning tokens, effectively hiding their decision process. The time horizon metric, developed by METR, quantifies the human-equivalent task length an AI can complete, gauging its autonomous capability.
Key Details
The study uses two metrics: 50% task-completion time horizon (TH) based on human time, and 50% reasoning token horizon based on o3-mini tokens. Projections suggest that by 2028, no-CoT TH could surpass 7 minutes, and by 2030, exceed 25 minutes. The evaluation spans math, coding, puzzles, causality, theory-of-mind, and strategic reasoning.

Study: FP8 on AI GPUs Matches Native FP64 HPC Performance

rss ArXiv cs.AI · 2026-06-08 · ⌛ 5 min read
Reason：Challenges a foundational belief in HPC by demonstrating that FP8 on AI-optimized GPUs can deliver full FP64 accuracy, potentially reshaping investments and hardware strategies in scientific computing.
📰 What's New
Researchers demonstrated that on AI-optimized GPUs like NVIDIA's B300, the Ozaki Scheme II leverages FP8 tensor throughput to emulate FP64 operations at full accuracy, achieving up to 500 TFLOPS—over an order of magnitude faster than the GPU's native FP64 peak of ~1.3 TFLOPS. The method matches or exceeds an H100 baseline across all tested HPC kernels, whereas using native FP64 on the B300 results in up to 50× slowdown. A companion technique using fixed-point reconstruction on INT32 pipes (Kulisch method) handles FFT operations, ensuring all kernel classes reach memory-roof performance at full FP64.
💡 Why It Matters： This challenges the long-held belief that native FP64 hardware is essential for scientific computing. If FP8-based emulation delivers full accuracy with higher performance, HPC centers could prioritize cost-effective AI GPUs over expensive double-precision hardware, reshaping procurement strategies and architectural roadmaps. It also enables energy-efficient simulation on a wider range of platforms.
📊 Key Facts: 1.3
Background
FP64 (double-precision floating-point) is the standard for scientific computing due to its 64-bit precision, but native FP64 hardware units are large and power-hungry. In contrast, FP8 (8-bit floating-point) is a low-precision format designed for AI training and inference, offering much higher throughput on specialized tensor cores. The Ozaki scheme is a known method for high-accuracy summation using lower-precision arithmetic, and the Chinese Remainder Theorem allows exact reconstruction of high-precision results from multiple low-precision computations. The B300 (Blackwell Ultra) is a recent AI GPU from NVIDIA that drastically reduced native FP64 units to favor FP8 tensor cores, causing concern in HPC that it would be unsuitable for double-precision workloads.
Key Details
The core technique, Ozaki Scheme II, applies the Chinese Remainder Theorem to decompose high-precision matrix multiplications into multiple FP8 tensor operations, combined with register-level fusion to eliminate memory overhead. The researchers introduce the Tensor-Memory Equilibrium (TME) model, extending the Roofline model with compute multiplier α, bandwidth multiplier β, and reconstruction latency γ. For the B300, β→1 makes emulation essentially free behind the memory wall. The emulated FP64 performance on B300 reaches ~500 TFLOPS for compute-bound kernels, while the Rubin R200 achieves ~400 TFLOPS.

Native3D: First End-to-End 3D Scene Generation Without 2D Intermediates

rss ArXiv cs.AI · 2026-06-08 · ⌛ 4 min read
Reason：Novel end-to-end 3D scene generation framework that bypasses 2D representations, addressing geometry-texture inconsistency; represents a significant technical advance in AI-generated 3D content.
📰 What's New
A research team introduced Native3D, the first end-to-end 3D scene generation framework that completely bypasses 2D intermediate representations. Traditional methods adapt 3D data to 2D for pre-trained diffusion models, causing geometric structural distortion and texture degradation. Native3D uses a unified mesh-texture joint representation processed by a Transformer-based scene encoder, simultaneously modeling geometry and texture while preserving spatial relationships and visual consistency. The framework also features a 3D Representation Alignment Loss (3D REPA Loss) that employs contrastive learning to align multi-level semantic features, significantly boosting geometric and textural fidelity.
💡 Why It Matters： By eliminating the 2D bottleneck, Native3D resolves the persistent geometry-texture inconsistency in AI-generated 3D content. This advance could accelerate high-fidelity 3D scene creation for gaming, film, and VR/AR, while opening up more flexible editing capabilities, potentially reducing manual effort in content pipelines.
Background
Conventional 3D scene pipelines often project 3D data into 2D images to leverage powerful diffusion models, then reconstruct 3D from the generated 2D views, which can introduce geometric errors and texture misalignment. Native3D's end-to-end 3D-native approach works directly on 3D representations, avoiding these issues. Contrastive learning is a self-supervised technique that pulls representations of similar data points together while pushing apart those of dissimilar ones, learning effective data representations.
Key Details
The unified mesh-texture joint representation captures geometry and texture in a single 3D-native format, avoiding separate generation steps. A Transformer-based encoder processes this representation, and the 3D REPA Loss uses contrastive learning to project multi-level semantic features into a shared latent space for better alignment. Experiments show Native3D outperforms existing methods in both generation quality and editing flexibility.

It includes FormalAgentLib, an extensible library that formally verifies semantic consistency of agent workflows under explicit assumptions and localizes failures in execution trajectories. On hard subsets of SWE-Bench-Verified and ELAIP-Bench, verification-passing workflows outperformed failing ones by 11.94% on average across five leading LLMs. The LeanEvolve method further improved SWE performance by 7.47% on average.
💡 Why It Matters： Most AI agent systems lack formal methods, leaving multi-step workflows vulnerable to unpredictable failures. 📊 Key Facts: 11.94% 7.47%
Background

Dependent types allow types to depend on values, enabling precise specifications. Formal methods have rarely been applied to AI agents despite their complexity, but the rise of multi-step LLM-based agents creates an urgent need for such reliability guarantees.
Key Details
FormalAgentLib models agent workflows as Lean4 terms, enabling machine-checked proofs of semantic consistency and using trajectory data to pinpoint failures. LeanEvolve then automatically revises workflows based on verification results. The framework was evaluated on hard subsets of SWE-Bench-Verified and ELAIP-Bench with five leading LLMs.
