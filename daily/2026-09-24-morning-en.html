Skill Habit Formation Makes AI Agents Consistent on Repeated Tasks

rss ArXiv cs.AI · 2026-09-24 · ⌛ 5 min read
Reason：Presents empirical evidence of significant agent inconsistency and wasteful re-planning, and proposes a novel skill habit formation method to improve reliability and efficiency, which is highly relevant for AI agent deployment. No community discussion provided to assess engagement.
📰 What's New
Researchers ran 42 tasks three times each and found that 38% to 74% of answers produced by different models did not agree. The same experiments showed 95.3% to 97.2% of an agent's generated output was spent re-deriving a plan the system already knew. To fix this, they proposed skill habit formation, where an agent mines its execution history for deterministic skill variants that handle common cases as scripts and fall through to reasoning for edge cases. On text-to-SQL, a habit-formed variant reproduced all 456 repeated dispatches, was non-inferior to every replaced reasoning arm (p<0.0001), and used 14% to 56% fewer tokens, becoming net positive after 7 to 53 reuses.
💡 Why It Matters： Consistency is a requirement for buyers, auditors, and regulators, so this method directly addresses a barrier to enterprise deployment of AI agents. By converting common cases into deterministic scripts, organizations can cut inference cost and make agent behavior auditable and reproducible. However, deterministic errors repeat exactly, meaning a flawed habit is as reliable as a good one, so guard mechanisms and boundary testing become critical for trustworthy automation.
📊 Key Facts: 38% 74% 95.3%
Background
AI agents built on large language models typically re-plan each task from scratch, which introduces both non-determinism and wasted computation. Text-to-SQL is the task of translating natural-language questions into database queries, a common benchmark for agent reliability. Skill habit formation is an approach that extracts reusable deterministic routines from an agent's past executions, analogous to how humans turn repeated actions into habits while still reasoning through unfamiliar cases.
Key Details
The proposed framework uses four gates of ascending cost; the central gate compares a candidate's execution trace against a retained reference within a tolerance derived from that reference's own run-to-run variability. Candidates declare the region of input space they claim, enabling common cases to execute as scripts while edge cases fall through to general reasoning. Separating routing from parameter extraction raised end-to-end accuracy from 0.888 to 0.952 at 43% of the cost, but the guard admitted 2.6% of natural paraphrases and 26% of inputs near its boundary, and 11 of 13 such failures were invisible to the trace-conformance gate at any threshold.

RAG-NAROK: Retrieval-Aware Poisoning Attack Targets RAG Systems with Source-Specific Refutation

rss ArXiv cs.AI · 2026-09-24 · ⌛ 5 min read
Reason：Presents a novel retrieval-aware poisoning attack framework targeting RAG systems with high technical depth and relevance for AI security practitioners, though no community discussion or engagement signals are provided.
📰 What's New
Published as arXiv:2609.25469, the paper by Abdullahil Kafi and Alvi Ataur Khalil introduces RAG-NAROK (Retrieval-Anchored Generation Negation And Response Quality Collapse), a retrieval-aware adversarial attack targeting retrieval augmented generation (RAG) systems. Unlike static poisoning attacks, RAG-NAROK extracts legitimate source identities from the retrieval pipeline and generates Anchor-Specific Refutation documents that explicitly name and devalue retrieved sources. It exploits recency and authority biases to steer text generation toward a target answer, and the authors report it significantly outperforms static baselines across diverse domains.
💡 Why It Matters： RAG systems are widely deployed to ground LLM outputs in external knowledge, but this attack demonstrates that their retrieval transparency can be weaponized to inject targeted misinformation even when individual sources appear legitimate. Security practitioners must now consider retrieval-aware dynamic poisoning as a distinct threat model, potentially requiring redesign of retrieval, ranking, and context assembly defenses.
Background
Retrieval augmented generation (RAG) lets LLMs fetch external documents at inference time and incorporate them into answers, reducing hallucination but expanding the attack surface. Traditional knowledge-base poisoning attacks insert pre-computed adversarial documents regardless of which sources the system retrieves, making them 'blind' to the query-specific context. RAG-NAROK is notable because it adapts its poisoned documents to the exact sources retrieved for each query, exploiting the transparency of the retrieval pipeline.
Key Details
RAG-NAROK operates in two stages: first, it queries the victim RAG pipeline to identify which legitimate sources will be retrieved for a given query; second, it generates Anchor-Specific Refutation documents that cite those sources by name and explicitly contradict or devalue them. The attack leverages recency and authority biases common in retrieval and generation stages, and the framework name stands for Retrieval-Anchored Generation Negation And Response Quality Collapse.

KEX-bench evaluates coding agents on kernel exploit primitive generation

rss ArXiv cs.AI · 2026-09-24 · ⌛ 4 min read
Reason：Introduces KEX-bench, a novel benchmark for evaluating coding agents on kernel exploit generation, revealing significant capability gaps and providing a valuable resource for AI security research. No community discussion provided.
📰 What's New
Researchers introduced KEX-bench, a benchmark of 45 task instances across 40 Linux and Windows CVEs, for evaluating coding agents on kernel exploit primitive generation. The benchmark covers kernel address leak, instruction-pointer control, heap read, heap write, and arbitrary address write. Without a reference PoC, the strongest configuration solves 14 of 25 Linux tasks (56.0%) and 1 of 20 Windows tasks (5.0%); with a reference PoC, it solves 31 of 45 tasks (68.9%).
💡 Why It Matters： The results show that general coding agents can reach kernel crashes but struggle to shape kernel state into exploitable primitives, especially on Windows. The 51-point gap between Linux and Windows no-PoC success rates suggests OS-specific kernel mitigations and attack surfaces heavily influence agent capability, providing a quantified signal for red-team tooling and defensive security assessments.
📊 Key Facts: 56.0% 5.0% 68.9%
Background
Kernel exploitation targets vulnerabilities in the operating system kernel, where a successful exploit typically needs to achieve an exploit primitive such as reading or writing kernel memory or controlling the instruction pointer before escalating privileges or executing code. Coding agents are AI systems that write and execute code; evaluating them on exploit generation tests whether they can go beyond bug detection to construct functional attack primitives. A reference proof of concept provides prior human exploit knowledge, and removing it forces agents to derive exploitation strategy from scratch.
Key Details
Each task runs in an isolated virtual machine with a controlled toolset and uses a deterministic verifier to check primitive-specific success. Agents are evaluated under fixed tool-call budgets across frontier and open-weight models. The benchmark is released at https://kex-bench.github.io for reproducible AI-assisted exploitation research.

Null-Basis LoRA Preserves Reasoning During Post-RL LLM Fine-Tuning

rss ArXiv cs.AI · 2026-09-24 · ⌛ 5 min read
Reason：Presents a novel parameter-efficient fine-tuning method (Null-Basis LoRA) that preserves reasoning abilities in post-RL LLMs, addressing a critical practical challenge for AI practitioners. Technical depth is high, but no community discussion or engagement signals are provided.
📰 What's New
Wenzhi Fang and three co-authors introduced Null-Basis Low-Rank Adaptation (NB-LoRA), a parameter-efficient fine-tuning method for adapting post-RL LLMs without overwriting reasoning. The method exploits the observation that reasoning activations concentrate in low-dimensional subspaces, leaving substantial null-space capacity for adaptation. It formulates reasoning retention as a layer-wise hidden-state preservation constraint and constructs a fixed approximate null basis from reasoning activations; LoRA updates are then reparameterized through this basis to enforce preservation. Across multiple RL-trained LLMs and downstream tasks, NB-LoRA matches standard LoRA adaptation performance, maintains reasoning accuracy near pre-fine-tuning levels, and generalizes to held-out reasoning benchmarks.
💡 Why It Matters： This addresses a critical problem for practitioners: post-RL models lose reasoning when supervised fine-tuning adapts them to new domains, forcing a tradeoff between adaptation quality and reasoning safety. NB-LoRA offers a low-overhead parameter-efficient method that preserves reasoning, enabling enterprises to customize reasoning models for vertical applications without expensive replay or retraining. It directly benefits AI teams deploying RL-trained LLMs in high-stakes fields such as healthcare, finance, and legal services.
Background
Reinforcement learning post-training uses reward signals to elicit step-by-step reasoning in LLMs. LoRA is a parameter-efficient fine-tuning method that trains low-rank matrices instead of full model parameters. Subsequent supervised fine-tuning can overwrite acquired reasoning behaviors, causing catastrophic forgetting. The null space of activations refers to directions that do not affect model outputs; updating parameters in these directions allows adaptation while preserving learned reasoning. NB-LoRA exploits this space to achieve both adaptation and retention.
Key Details
NB-LoRA formulates reasoning retention as a layer-wise hidden-state preservation constraint and estimates approximate null spaces from reasoning activations. LoRA updates are reparameterized through a fixed null basis, avoiding the substantial training overhead of gradient projection methods. Experiments show adaptation performance equal to standard LoRA, reasoning accuracy near pre-fine-tuning levels, and generalization to held-out reasoning benchmarks.

Synthetic Personas Fail to Predict Real Audience Click-Through in Upworthy A/B Study

rss ArXiv cs.AI · 2026-09-24 · ⌛ 4 min read
Reason：Rigorous empirical study with counterintuitive finding that persona-based LLM simulation underperforms a simpler no-persona baseline, valuable for AI practitioners in marketing and adtech; no community discussion available.
📰 What's New
A sim-to-real validity study using the Upworthy Research Archive—thousands of headline A/B tests on real traffic with measured click-through—compared a ten-persona panel grounded in real audience demographics against a no-persona zero-shot baseline. On the reliable subset (n=399), the no-persona baseline ranked variants markedly better: Kendall τ=0.361 and top-1 accuracy 49.2%, versus τ=0.084 and top-1 34.6% for the persona panel, with non-overlapping confidence intervals. The finding replicated across three independent Upworthy splits, a different-domain news dataset, three Gemini tiers, and OpenAI gpt-4.1. The direct no-persona model tapped an accurate population-level prior, while role-playing personas injected bias and noise.
💡 Why It Matters： For marketers and adtech practitioners using LLM synthetic personas to predict audience response, this study indicates that persona conditioning degrades predictive validity compared to a simpler zero-shot baseline. It challenges the premise that profile-conditioned LLMs effectively mimic human samples, and may push teams toward lightweight ranking models rather than elaborate persona simulations for predicting aggregate engagement.
📊 Key Facts: 49.2% 34.6% 0.361
Background
The Upworthy Research Archive is a public dataset of thousands of real-traffic headline A/B tests with measured click-through rates. Synthetic personas are LLM prompts that specify demographic profiles to simulate audience reactions; Kendall τ is a rank correlation statistic used to measure how well predicted rankings match observed outcomes.
Key Details
The reliable subset included 399 A/B tests with statistically distinguishable winners; ranking was evaluated with Kendall τ and top-1 accuracy. The no-persona baseline asked how likely a typical reader is to click, while the persona panel used ten demographic-grounded profiles. Cross-model tests covered three Gemini tiers and OpenAI gpt-4.1, with a public artifact-first replication package.
