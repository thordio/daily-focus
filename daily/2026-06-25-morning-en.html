VLMs Mimic Human Visual Search Using Reasoning Tokens

rss ArXiv cs.AI · 2026-06-25 · ⌛ 4 min read
Reason：Introduces a novel method using reasoning tokens as a reaction-time analog to probe vision-language model behavior, bridging cognitive science and AI evaluation with potential impact on benchmarking.
📰 What's New
A researcher adapted four classic visual-search paradigms—feature vs. conjunction, spatial configuration, enumeration, and search asymmetry—to test vision-language models (VLMs). The study used the number of reasoning tokens as a surrogate for reaction time and compared model behavior to a large human benchmark from Wolfe et al. (2010). Models reproduced human signatures: feature search showed flat effort while conjunction search effort increased with set size. However, target-present effort slopes exceeded target-absent slopes, reversing the human pattern.
💡 Why It Matters： This approach bridges decades of psychophysics research with AI evaluation, offering a sharp and inexpensive probe into machine visual cognition. It reveals both parallels and divergences, indicating where VLMs process information like humans and where they follow distinct computational strategies. The findings can improve model interpretability and guide the development of more human-aligned AI systems.
Background
Visual search paradigms are standard tasks in cognitive psychology where subjects find a target among distractors; reaction time reveals whether search is parallel (pop-out) or serial (attention-demanding). Since VLMs lack reaction time, this study uses reasoning tokens—extra tokens generated during thinking—as a proxy for cognitive effort. The human benchmark from Wolfe et al. (2010) provides large-scale normative data on search slopes.
Key Details
The study tested frontier and mid-tier models, using resolution controls to confirm that conjunction cost arises from genuine search rather than difficulty resolving small shapes. A reasoning model with adaptive deliberation refused to spend reasoning tokens on detection tasks, leading to an effort gradient in one model and an accuracy cliff in another. Enumeration tasks remained accurate where humans typically lose count.

Clinicians Demand Confidence Gates in Autonomous AI Prescribing

rss ArXiv cs.AI · 2026-06-25 · ⌛ 4 min read
Reason：Addresses critical safety and trust issues for autonomous AI prescribing with empirical survey evidence; relevant to regulatory and technical standards but lacks market-moving immediacy.
📰 What's New
A survey of 136 U.S. prescribing clinicians found they would not permit autonomous AI prescribing without a calibrated per-prediction confidence-based escalation mechanism. When uncertainty is epistemic (model ignorance), clinicians preferred abstention; for aleatoric (clinical ambiguity) uncertainty, they favored a competing-options summary. The paper proposes three minimum architectural requirements: calibrated confidence, differentiated communication of uncertainty types, and inferential transparency for liability allocation. These features effectively reduce the system's autonomy, turning it into a heavily supervised decision-support tool.
💡 Why It Matters： The findings directly address recent legislative developments like the U.S. H.R. 238 bill and Utah’s prescription-renewal pilot, which authorize AI to prescribe independently. The proposed requirements offer regulators concrete technical standards to constrain AI autonomy and align liability with system designers and deployers. This framework could shape healthcare AI market access and increase clinical adoption by preserving meaningful human oversight.
Background
Epistemic uncertainty stems from model ignorance or incomplete data and can be reduced with more data; aleatoric uncertainty is inherent randomness or clinical ambiguity that cannot be eliminated. H.R. 238 is a U.S. federal bill proposing to grant AI systems authority to prescribe medications without direct physician oversight; Utah’s pilot program allows AI to renew prescriptions within limited boundaries.
Key Details
The three architectural requirements: (1) calibrated per-prediction confidence to gate autonomous actions, (2) differentiated handling of epistemic versus aleatoric uncertainty—epistemic triggers abstention, aleatoric presents competing options, (3) inferential transparency enabling substantive clinical judgment under uncertainty, which is the sole condition under which clinicians accepted additional liability.

📰 What's New
On June 24, embodied AI startup RoboScience fully disclosed its self-developed Visics universal embodied large model and the VLOA (Vision-Language-Object-Action) architecture for the first time. The model introduces Object Trajectory—a 3D point cloud trajectory representation of objects—as a unified intermediate standard. It features a dual-engine design: an embodied world model pre-trained on massive internet videos to learn real-world object motion laws, and a general manipulation model trained on synthetic data from a physics engine to generate robot control commands. RoboScience also built a simulation-plus-video data flywheel that reduces per-data-point cost to 1/20–1/200 of traditional methods, with plans to accumulate over 1TB of high-quality manipulation trajectory data by 2026. 💡 Why It Matters： By defining a shared object-centric representation, Visics tackles three core bottlenecks in embodied AI: poor generalization, difficulty in fine-grained manipulation, and error accumulation in long-horizon tasks. Background
The embodied AI field currently lacks a standard foundational representation unit—most approaches directly map joint trajectories to specific robot hardware, preventing skill transfer to new embodiments or tasks.
