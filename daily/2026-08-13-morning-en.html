LLM Conformity Mitigations Lie on a Resistance-Receptivity Frontier

rss ArXiv cs.AI · 2026-08-13 · ⌛ 4 min read
Reason：Novel empirical study quantifying conformity in multi-LLM collaboration, revealing a key trade-off between resistance and receptivity; important for practitioners building robust multi-agent systems. No discussion comments provided.
📰 What's New
Researchers tested 23 open-weight LLMs across 19 conditions and three datasets, grading more than one million responses. A unanimous wrong majority reversed 22.8% of correct MMLU answers, 54.8% on GPQA, and 71.0% on SimpleQA. Between 84% and 89% of reversed answers matched the peers' incorrect responses. They introduce Resistance and Receptivity as paired metrics, and find six mitigation methods fall on a single frontier with R² 0.80 to 0.90.
💡 Why It Matters： For multi-agent AI systems, this trade-off means any mitigation that makes a model resist wrong peers also makes it less likely to accept correct corrections from peers. Practitioners cannot simply maximize resistance; they need to choose a point on the frontier based on task requirements. The finding that reasoning improves both metrics on self-derivable MMLU subjects suggests architecture or training choices can break the trade-off for some tasks.
📊 Key Facts: 22.8% 54.8% 71.0%
Background
In multi-LLM collaboration, each agent sees peer assertions before answering, so social conformity can override parametric knowledge. Resistance measures how often a model keeps its correct answer under peer pressure; Receptivity measures how often it adopts a correct peer answer after initially being wrong. Prior conformity mitigations typically optimized only Resistance, which this study shows is insufficient because a collaborating agent must also accept useful corrections. MMLU, GPQA, and SimpleQA are benchmarks for general knowledge, graduate-level science, and factual short-answer questions respectively.
Key Details
The study scores six methods on Resistance and Receptivity, four from prior work and two newly introduced. Reflection, the strongest published method, gains 7.9 points of MMLU Resistance but loses 15.3 points of Receptivity. Reasoning is the exception: on GPQA and SimpleQA it trades like the rest, but on MMLU subjects where a model can derive answers itself it raises Resistance by 7.2 and Receptivity by 9.6 simultaneously.

Deployment Decision Reliability framework reveals agent leaderboards rank specialization not capability

rss ArXiv cs.AI · 2026-08-13 · ⌛ 5 min read
Reason：The paper provides a rigorous, generalizability-theory-based analysis showing that agent leaderboards rank specialization rather than general capability, with significant implications for enterprise AI deployment decisions. It challenges the validity of common benchmark evaluations, making it highly relevant for AI practitioners and investors relying on these metrics. No comments provided.
📰 What's New
Researchers analyzed three open agent-trace benchmarks—TheAgentCompany, τ²-bench, and AppWorld—using a four-facet Generalizability Theory variance decomposition. Across every dataset and check type, the agent main effect accounted for less than 3% of total variance, while agent-by-task interaction accounted for 7–23%. Three estimators—Henderson Method-I, REML via lme4, and a Bayesian binomial GLMM—agreed to three decimal places. On τ² action_checks, aggregate reliability Eρ² fell from 0.752 to 0.000 on the hardest task quartile. Training-cell reliability negatively correlated with held-out reliability at r = -0.90.
💡 Why It Matters： Enterprise buyers that read agent leaderboards as capability rankings risk selecting agents that have only specialized to benchmark tasks. The study finds population-level diagnostics such as capability-gap ratio transfer across enterprise benchmarks at 0.35–0.40, but per-family agent rankings invert, so rank order cannot guide deployment. DDR gives a one-page reporting discipline with five defensible decisions.
📊 Key Facts: 3% 23% 0.752
Background
Generalizability Theory extends classical test theory by partitioning observed score variance into multiple facets such as persons, tasks, and raters, enabling reliability estimates for specific generalization contexts. Long-horizon agent benchmarks like TheAgentCompany, τ²-bench, and AppWorld present agents with multi-step enterprise tasks checked by environment verifiers. The reliability coefficient Eρ² measures how well an observed score generalizes to a universe of similar tasks and checks. The MAST failure taxonomy classifies agent failures into categories to compare failure modes.
Key Details
The four-facet decomposition attributes variance to agent, task, check type, and interactions; agent-by-task interaction dominates over the agent main effect. On the MAST failure taxonomy, trace-level mode profiles are idiosyncratic with MAE = 0.261, while cell-level profiles generalize with MAE = 0.056 and r = 0.83. All code, data loaders, and fit artifacts are released under an open-source license.

📰 What's New
Researchers transplanted weights from aligned models into matched unaligned base models across two open-weight model pairs and four safety benchmarks. Replacing MLP parameters recovered at least 2.7 times more malicious-prompt refusal than replacing attention parameters. Within the MLP stack, the block spanning layers 8-11 was selected first in all six greedy searches over model-dataset pairs. In five of six greedy trajectories, adding more aligned blocks reduced refusal performance, showing non-additive composition.
💡 Why It Matters： Safety alignment in current LLMs is not uniformly distributed; it can be manipulated by editing a small set of mid-network MLP parameters. This lowers the cost of alignment audits, adversarial removal, and targeted red-teaming, while also explaining observed brittleness. The benchmark-dependent precision-coverage trade-off means refusal evaluation must account for which benchmark is used to derive interventions.
📊 Key Facts: 2.7
Background
In transformer language models, MLP layers are feedforward blocks that transform token representations, while attention layers compute pairwise token interactions. Safety alignment usually fine-tunes a pre-trained base model to refuse harmful prompts. Weight transplant experiments copy parameters from an aligned model into an unaligned base model to identify which components carry refusal behavior.
Key Details
The study uses two open-weight model pairs and four safety benchmarks, including OR-Bench. Greedy search consistently selected the block spanning layers 8-11 first. Selective block subsets outperformed full MLP transplantation on malicious refusal, benign over-refusal, or both in five of six trajectories.

Reinforcement Learning Cuts GPU Power Violations 89.8% in LLM Training

rss ArXiv cs.AI · 2026-08-13 · ⌛ 5 min read
Reason：Presents novel technical work on RL-based power control for LLM training, with concrete measurements and energy efficiency gains at smaller scales, but reveals actuator authority limitations at 72B scale, making it an important but not yet fully solved contribution to AI datacenter efficiency.
📰 What's New
Researchers instrumented GRPO training with half-second power telemetry on 7B, 14B, and 72B models running on one to four A100 GPUs, collecting over 380,000 samples. They trained a PPO meta-controller that adjusts generation parameters in response to measured power. On a full 500-step 7B trace, the controller reduced power-limit violations by 89.8%, increased token output by 18.1%, and improved energy efficiency by 26.2% tokens per MWh. Live deployment at 72B initially failed because the group-size actuator lost authority under model sharding; a rebuilt controller using generation concurrency as actuator produced 35.7% more output than a static safe baseline with 2.27±1.08% budget violations across three replications.
💡 Why It Matters： For AI datacenter operators, the results suggest that RL-based power control can enable roughly twofold oversubscription of GPU nameplate power without violating limits at 30-second or longer measurement windows, potentially cutting infrastructure and energy costs. The 72B live deployment demonstrates that actuator choice is critical under model sharding, pointing to a path for large-scale LLM training fleets to improve throughput and carbon efficiency.
📊 Key Facts: 89.8% 18.1% 26.2%
Background
GRPO (Group Relative Policy Optimization) is a reinforcement-learning post-training method for LLMs that generates multiple responses per prompt and updates the policy based on relative rewards. PPO (Proximal Policy Optimization) is a reinforcement-learning algorithm used here to train the meta-controller that modulates generation parameters. Model sharding splits a large model across multiple GPUs, which can change how much influence a parameter such as group size has on total power draw. Actuator authority refers to how much a controllable parameter can change measured power; in this work, generation concurrency is shown to retain authority at 72B where group size does not.
Key Details
The meta-controller uses PPO to map measured power to workload generation parameters. An actuator-authority sweep found that applying the same parameters as generation concurrency retains 17–22% power authority at 72B, whereas the original group-size actuator lost authority under model sharding. At realistic measurement windows, original 72B power transients fall from 23.6% at half-second resolution to 1.6% at 30 seconds and zero at 5 minutes; a composed 16-GPU fleet showed zero violations at 30s and longer, with peak demand at 50–56% of nameplate.

Background
Activation steering modifies a language model's behavior by adding a learned direction vector to its hidden activations, enabling targeted changes without retraining. It is used for alignment and capability control but can produce unintended changes in unrelated behaviors. The paper studies whether these side effects can be forecasted before steering is applied.
Key Details

Direction prediction leverages the model's unsteered hidden representations, achieving substantially higher accuracy than simple baselines, while magnitude is mostly determined by the target behavior rather than the source behavior.

LinearKV Uses One Cached State for Position-Independent Caching in Hybrid LLMs

rss ArXiv cs.AI · 2026-08-13 · ⌛ 5 min read
Reason：Novel training-free method enabling position-independent caching for hybrid LLMs, addressing a practical inference acceleration gap. Technical depth is high, but no community discussion signals are provided to assess broader validation or engagement.
📰 What's New
LinearKV introduced a training-free decoupled initialization technique that maps each linear layer's K matched local states to a single initial state, while full-attention layers concatenate KV entries as before. Across three hybrid models and three PIC selectors, on two GDN models the single-state and exact composition approaches both recover up to 92% of full quality. On the Mamba-2 model, exact composition recovers only 46.6% of full quality under EPIC, versus 86.8% for a single cached block initializer. The single state initializer also reduces time-to-first-token to 0.46× full prefill, while exact composition adds 5–17% overhead; results hold on LongBench QA and RULER at 8K–32K.
💡 Why It Matters： Hybrid LLMs replace most attention layers with linear recurrences to reduce memory, but existing position-independent caching methods assume token-indexed KV caches and cannot be applied directly. LinearKV removes that barrier without retraining, enabling serving systems to reuse cached states and cut time-to-first-token to 0.46× full prefill. This directly benefits inference providers deploying models like Mamba-2 and GDN for long-context workloads where full prefill is expensive.
📊 Key Facts: 92% 46.6% 86.8%
Background
Position-independent caching (PIC) accelerates LLM serving by matching reusable token chunks, concatenating their KV entries, and selectively recomputing a few tokens to restore cross-chunk context; it is built for full-attention models with token-indexed KV caches. Hybrid LLMs replace most attention layers with linear recurrences that expose only a fixed-size state, eliminating token-indexed KV and breaking those primitives. HYPIC is concurrent work that attempts exact composition of cached states for hybrid models. LinearKV instead shows a single cached state initializer is sufficient and cheaper.
Key Details
LinearKV's decoupled initialization maps each linear layer's K matched local states to a single initial state, while full-attention layers concatenate their KV as before; this reuses existing PIC token selection and recomputation unchanged. A single cached state suffices as the linear layer initializer, while composing all K cached states into the exact full-prefix state—as concurrent work HYPIC does—is unnecessary and on Mamba-2 harmful. Under the EPIC selector, exact composition recovers 46.6% of full quality on Mamba-2 versus 86.8% for the single state, and exact composition adds 5–17% overhead on top of the 0.46× full prefill baseline.
