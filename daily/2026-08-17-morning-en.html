📰 What's New
HELIX introduces a source-traceable substrate for recursive self-improvement by co-evolving agent models and runtime harnesses. It decomposes agent systems into typed ports, reusable atoms, recipes, product shells, and runtime policies, while preserving intervention identity and provenance. In one code-repair evolution round, a 65-candidate portfolio discovers a fixed harness that improves task coverage by 4.0% over Pi; the full portfolio exposes up to 58.0% more verified coverage through complementary sibling behavior. Background
In agentic AI, a model's runtime harness mediates context, tools, control flow, and stopping, determining what the model can accomplish and which trajectories become learning data. Recursive self-improvement typically focuses on updating the model, but HELIX co-evolves the harness with the model: build harnesses for a fixed model, update the model from verified sibling trajectories, then rebuild harnesses as model capabilities change. This harness-in-the-loop perspective treats execution scaffolds as data-generating components, not just inference-time tools.

📰 What's New
An arXiv paper (2608.13607) evaluated seven regression signals—confidence, logit margin, attention entropy, output KL divergence, likelihood drift, token-level KL, and representation drift—across six benchmarks and six LLM update pairs. It found that signal effectiveness is task-dependent: confidence is strongest on multiple-choice QA and simpler math, while likelihood/KL signals give the most frequent gains on harder math and code generation. No signal is universally best across model updates. Some cross-version signals remain informative even when confidence fails and require no labels, supporting a proof-of-concept selective fallback that routes high-risk samples back to the old model.
💡 Why It Matters： The findings give practitioners a task-level guide for choosing regression signals after LLM updates, reducing blind reliance on confidence scores. Deployment teams monitoring model reliability can use likelihood/KL signals for math and code tasks and confidence for MCQ tasks. The proof-of-concept selective fallback demonstrates how high-risk samples can be routed to an older model without label costs.
Background
Frontier LLM updates generally improve aggregate performance but can regress on individual samples, where a previously correct answer becomes incorrect under the new model. Sample-level regression is hard to detect because aggregate metrics hide per-sample failures. Existing monitoring often relies on confidence scores, but this paper shows confidence is not universally reliable across tasks and updates.
Key Details
The unified added-value test isolates each signal's gain over a confidence baseline. The seven signals span single-model signals (confidence, logit margin, attention entropy) and cross-version signals (output KL divergence, likelihood drift, token-level KL, representation drift). Probabilistic JEPA Shown Equivalent to Hidden Markov Models; MCJEPA Introduced

rss ArXiv cs.AI · 2026-08-17 · ⌛ 6 min read
Reason：The paper provides a rigorous theoretical connection between JEPA and hidden Markov models, introducing MCJEPA with exact multi-horizon consistency. This is high-value for AI researchers working on predictive architectures and world models, though no community discussion or engagement signals are available to validate broader impact.
📰 What's New
Researchers established a formal equivalence between probabilistic Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPA) and hidden Markov models (HMMs). Full time-indexed Predictive Information Bottleneck VJEPA (PIB-VJEPA) exhibits the same computational structure: a stochastic context encoder acts as an amortized filtering distribution, a probabilistic predictor defines latent-state dynamics, and a decoder, inverse target encoder, or induced implicit conditional supplies the emission direction. They introduce Markov-Chain JEPA (MCJEPA), which replaces the latent predictor with a learned transition matrix; in the finite time-homogeneous case, matrix powers guarantee exact multi-horizon Chapman-Kolmogorov consistency. Four progressively stronger levels of correspondence and sufficient conditions for exact sequence-level HMM equivalence are defined.
💡 Why It Matters： For researchers building world models and predictive architectures, this equivalence provides a formal path to apply HMM filtering, state propagation, and multi-horizon consistency tools to JEPA, potentially improving reliability of predictions across time horizons. It may also yield stronger theoretical guarantees and training stability for JEPA-based systems in robotics, video prediction, and reinforcement learning.
Background
Hidden Markov models (HMMs) model sequential data through hidden states that evolve via Markov transitions and emit observations. Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPA) learn representations by predicting one view from another in latent space, commonly used for self-supervised learning and world models. The Chapman-Kolmogorov equations describe how transition probabilities compose over multiple time steps. The predictive information bottleneck principle balances compression of representations with preservation of predictive information.
Key Details
The correspondence spans four levels; PIB-VJEPA's stochastic context encoder corresponds to an amortized filtering distribution, its probabilistic predictor to latent-state dynamics, and its decoder, inverse target encoder, or induced implicit conditional to the emission direction. MCJEPA achieves exact multi-horizon Chapman-Kolmogorov consistency via matrix powers and extends to conditioned discrete-state transitions, continuous-state Markov kernels, and continuous-time dynamics; deterministic temporal JEPA appears as a degenerate Dirac-kernel special case. Predictive information-bottleneck learning is interpreted as seeking a compact predictive state, where compression promotes minimality and residual predictability tests sufficiency.
