Google's DeepCrossAttention speeds transformer training up to 3x with depth-wise attention

hackernews ComputedCamp995 · 2026-11-27 · ⌛ 3 min read
Reason：Google Research's DeepCrossAttention introduces a novel depth-wise attention mechanism that achieves comparable model quality up to 3x faster, representing important architectural progress with strong community discussion (489 points, 174 comments) covering practical implications like model speed and quality.
📰 What's New
Google Research introduced DeepCrossAttention (DCA), a technique that applies cross-attention across network depth rather than across tokens. Language modeling experiments show that DCA achieves the same model quality up to 3x faster while adding a negligible number of parameters—about 0.2%. The work is presented in an ICML 2025 paper and described in arXiv preprint 2502.06785.
💡 Why It Matters： For LLM developers, cutting training time by 3x with almost no parameter overhead reduces compute cost and accelerates iteration cycles. This could make large-scale transformer training more accessible and shift architecture research toward depth-wise attention mechanisms.
📊 Key Facts: 0.2%
Background
Transformers process sequences using self-attention across tokens and pass information through stacked layers via residual connections. In standard models, each layer only sees the previous layer’s output; DeepCrossAttention changes this by letting each position attend across earlier layer representations. This depth-wise attention gives models access to historical computations without increasing sequence-length-related costs.
Key Details
DCA uses depth-wise cross-attention with split Q/K/V; each token queries a dynamically weighted mix of all prior layer embeddings, giving access to full historical context without the computational penalty of scaling standard attention. The arXiv paper reports improved perplexity for a given training time and max 3x speed with 0.2% added parameters.
