CellWorld: Latent Cell Prediction Scales Spatial Transcriptomics Foundation Models

rss ArXiv cs.AI · 2026-08-10 · ⌛ 5 min read
Reason：Proposes a novel pretraining objective that shifts from gene-level to latent cell prediction, addressing technical variation and enabling scalable foundation models; important methodological contribution for spatial transcriptomics.
📰 What's New
CellWorld introduces a new pretraining strategy for spatial transcriptomics foundation models, shifting from gene-level reconstruction to latent cell prediction. The model predicts latent representations of masked cells from spatial context and partial expression hints. Four model variants, ranging from 5.74 million to 94.56 million trainable parameters, were pretrained on a corpus of 46 million human cells. Even the smallest variant (5.74M parameters) outperforms all baselines on 11 linear-probe and 7 fine-tuned spatial benchmarks across four held-out datasets. A frozen CellWorld-Large pretrained on only 5% of the corpus with broad biological source coverage outperforms fully fine-tuned baselines on all seven spatial benchmarks.
💡 Why It Matters： This approach addresses a key limitation of existing methods: gene-level reconstruction can capture assay-specific technical variation, reducing transferability. By operating in latent space, CellWorld learns more robust cell representations that generalize across different spatial transcriptomics platforms. The finding that a model trained on just 5% of data can outperform fully fine-tuned baselines suggests efficient pretraining, potentially lowering computational costs for developing foundation models in biology.
📊 Key Facts: 5% 5.74 94.56
Background
Spatial transcriptomics measures gene expression within tissue, preserving spatial context. Foundation models for this domain typically use self-supervised pretraining on large cell datasets. Existing models often reconstruct gene expression values, which may inadvertently learn technical artifacts from different assays. Latent cell prediction shifts the prediction target to a learned representation space, aiming to be more invariant to such artifacts, thus improving transferability.
Key Details
The CellWorld architecture predicts latent cell representations from visible spatial neighbors and a partial hint of the cell's gene expression. Pretraining was performed using a masking strategy on 46 million human cells, with controlled scaling experiments examining model capacity, data diversity, and optimization. Performance improvements were observed with larger model sizes, particularly for spatial tasks, while spatial transfer depended more on optimization and biological diversity than on total cell count. The code is available at GitHub.

EntropyMoE: Entropy-Aware Sparse Routing for Tokenizer-Free LLMs

rss ArXiv cs.AI · 2026-08-10 · ⌛ 3 min read
Reason：The paper introduces an innovative integration of Mixture-of-Experts with entropy-based routing for dynamic byte patches in tokenizer-free LLMs. This represents a notable technical advance that could improve model efficiency and adaptability, though it is not a paradigm-shifting breakthrough.
📰 What's New
EntropyMoE introduces an entropy-aware sparse expert routing method for tokenizer-free byte-level large language models. It replaces the dense feed-forward modules in a global patch Transformer with Top-K expert layers, where each dynamic patch is routed to different experts based on its entropy. Evaluated on held-out data, EntropyMoE achieves the lowest bits-per-byte among both dense and sparse baselines while maintaining comparable downstream accuracy.
💡 Why It Matters： By using patch entropy to dynamically allocate computation, tokenizer-free models can adapt their capacity to the information density of patches, potentially improving efficiency on noisy text or multilingual tasks. It extends Mixture-of-Experts conditional computation beyond token-based representations, paving the way for more efficient universal language modeling.
Background
Tokenizer-free LLMs avoid fixed vocabularies by grouping bytes into dynamic patches, but previous architectures applied the same computation to every patch. Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) is a sparse conditional computation scheme where only a subset of experts are activated per input. Information entropy quantifies the randomness or information content of data, here used to gauge patch complexity.
Key Details
The router selects experts directly from patch entropy without a learned routing network; a patch's byte coverage determines its contribution to workload accounting; entropy and patch length jointly define the feature space for expert specialization.

The approach outperforms router-based, score-based, and sparse autoencoder-based interpretation methods in both automatic and human evaluations, achieving more coherent and faithful characterizations while maintaining competitive reward modeling accuracy.
💡 Why It Matters： This advance directly aids AI alignment in RLHF pipelines by providing faithful interpretation of reward models, which are critical for training large language models with human preferences. Background

In reward modeling for RLHF, MoE architectures aim to make the reward signal more interpretable. Previous methods used routing weights to assign prompts to experts, but those only show which prompts an expert receives, not how it evaluates responses. CoCo addresses this gap by directly examining expert contributions to reward scores for response pairs.
Key Details
CoCo operates on a response level, contrasting the reward contributions of each expert for chosen and rejected responses. It jointly models routing (which expert is activated) and preference (how the expert contributes to the reward gap). The method selects response pairs with the largest contribution contrasts to characterize each expert's specialization.

MAP Predicts Middle-Layer Attention to Prune Visual Tokens, Achieving 3x Speedup

rss ArXiv cs.AI · 2026-08-10 · ⌛ 5 min read
Reason：The paper addresses an important efficiency problem in multimodal LLMs with a novel prediction-based approach, making it valuable for practitioners working on model optimization, though it is an incremental advancement rather than a breakthrough.
📰 What's New
The paper introduces Middle-layer Attention Prediction (MAP) to address two issues in using fixed middle-layer attention for visual token pruning in MLLMs: the optimal layer varies per sample, and obtaining attention from that layer incurs significant pre-computation. MAP proposes Question Contrastive Teacher Selection to dynamically select a teacher layer for each sample by contrasting attention under original and reference questions. It then distills the selected layer's attention into a lightweight predictor that estimates visual token importance directly from multi-modal input features. On LLaVA-NeXT-7B across 10 benchmarks, MAP retains 97.5% of original performance using only 5.56% of visual tokens, yielding a 3.09x end-to-end speedup.
💡 Why It Matters： This approach drastically reduces inference cost for multimodal LLMs, making them more practical for deployment in resource-constrained settings and real-time applications. The dynamic layer selection improves pruning accuracy and sets a new direction for efficient model optimization.
📊 Key Facts: 97.5% 5.56% 3.09
Background
Multimodal large language models (MLLMs) process images as numerous visual tokens, which incur heavy computation. Visual token pruning discards redundant tokens guided by importance scores. Prior work used text-to-vision attention from a predefined middle language model layer as importance signal, but this fixed layer is suboptimal across different inputs and computing that attention still wastes resources. MAP predicts per-sample optimal attention to prune tokens more precisely and efficiently.
Key Details
Key technical elements: Question Contrastive Teacher Selection identifies the optimal layer by computing the attention difference between the original question and a meaningless reference question, selecting the layer with maximum discrepancy. The lightweight predictor operates on multi-modal features (e.g., vision encoder outputs) and requires no attention maps at inference. Pruning occurs before the first language model layer, with a diversity criterion to preserve varied information.

📊 Key Facts: 52.01% 7.88 44.953
Background
Reinforcement learning (RL) is a training method where models learn by receiving rewards for desired behaviors. In LLM-based web development, defining appropriate rewards is difficult because functional correctness requires executing and testing the generated website's interactive features. Traditional methods use hand-written browser scripts, which are expensive for diverse tasks, or vision-language model (VLM) graders that may give premature verdicts.
