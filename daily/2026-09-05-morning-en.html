Speculative Macro Commit Reduces Tool-Using LLM Agent Wall Time by up to 44.9%

rss ArXiv cs.AI · 2026-09-05 · ⌛ 5 min read
Reason：Novel technical mechanism addressing a key bottleneck (latency) in tool-using LLM agents; high technical depth and potential impact for practitioners, but preprint without community validation or shown empirical results keeps it from being breakthrough.
📰 What's New
Speculative Macro Commit (SMC) is a runtime mechanism that uses a two-tier agent system: a Qwen3.5-27B INT4 authoritative actor model and a Qwen3.5-4B speculative drafter model. The drafter continuously predicts and executes future action chains on an isolated environment snapshot, while SMC mines recurring multi-action skeletons from training traces and stores them in a macro library. On the τ²-Bench Telecom subset, SMC reduces latency by 10.23% over the Speculative Actions baseline and 18.59% over sequential execution; on AppWorld, wall time drops by 7.7% over SA and 44.9% over sequential, with a small reduction in task completion. The code is publicly available on GitHub.
💡 Why It Matters： Tool-using LLM agents often face significant wall-clock latency from serial action-observation loops, limiting their practicality in real-time applications. SMC's multi-step speculative execution reduces this latency by up to 44.9% without sacrificing overall accuracy on τ²-Bench, showing that macro-level commitment can extend speculative execution beyond single actions. This gives practitioners a concrete method to speed up autonomous agents in customer service, workflow automation, and other latency-sensitive tasks.
📊 Key Facts: 10.23% 18.59% 7.7%
Background
Tool-using LLM agents perform tasks by calling external tools (e.g., search, database queries) and waiting for observations after each call, which creates serial action-observation loops that dominate wall-clock time. Speculative execution uses a smaller, faster model to predict and run future actions ahead of time, allowing the large model to accept them if they match, thereby reducing idle wait. The Speculative Actions baseline only speculates one step at a time; SMC extends this to committing entire multi-step action chains.
Key Details
SMC maintains a macro library of recurring multi-action skeletons mined from training traces; at runtime, when the authoritative actor's next tool call matches the first drafted action, all remaining pre-executed draft steps and their observations are committed to the official trajectory. The system uses Qwen3.5-27B INT4 as the actor and Qwen3.5-4B as the drafter, with an isolated environment snapshot to ensure safety of speculative execution. Results show a 10.23% latency reduction over Speculative Actions on τ²-Bench Telecom and a 44.9% wall-time reduction over sequential on AppWorld.

rss ArXiv cs.AI · 2026-09-05 · ⌛ 4 min read
Reason：The paper introduces PlanFence, a novel dependency-scoped validation protocol addressing a practical consistency problem in distributed LLM-agent teams. It shows clear technical depth and experimental validation, making it relevant for AI practitioners working on multi-agent systems. However, it is a specific research contribution without broader market or strategic implications, and no community discussion or engagement signals are provided.
📰 What's New
PlanFence, a dependency-scoped action-validation protocol, prevents stale-plan execution in distributed LLM-agent teams. Plans cite the exact public records they used, and an executor validates only the records that can affect the pending external action. In 30 controlled live workflows with a post-plan revision, a freshness-only executor acts on the obsolete plan in every task, whereas PlanFence completes all tasks without an invalid action. Controlled replay reveals two conditional boundaries: proactive synchronization yields lower coordination stall at low churn, while PlanFence avoids repeated update-path coordination as churn grows and avoids validating unrelated state as the shared keyspace grows.
💡 Why It Matters： For AI practitioners building multi-agent systems, PlanFence addresses a critical consistency gap: state freshness does not guarantee plan validity. It enables safer external actions without full state synchronization, reducing coordination overhead in dynamic environments. The controlled results indicate where dependency-scoped validation is most cost-effective compared to proactive synchronization.
Background
Stale-plan execution occurs when a planner derives an action from one requirement, another agent commits a new requirement, and an executor receives the new state without replacing the old plan. State freshness only confirms the executor has the latest shared facts, not that the plan authorizing an action remains valid. PlanFence is a dependency-scoped validation protocol that checks only the records relevant to the pending action.
Key Details
PlanFence plans cite the exact public records they used, and an executor validates only the records that can affect the pending external action. If validation is incomplete, the executor replans once or blocks. Controlled replay boundaries show proactive synchronization has lower coordination stall at low churn, but PlanFence avoids repeated update-path coordination at higher churn and avoids validating unrelated state as shared keyspace grows.

📰 What's New
DuplexSpeechBench-IFEval is a new benchmark with 1,038 test cases spanning eight assistant roles and five conditioning protocols for evaluating implicit instruction-following in full-duplex voice agents. It uses a deterministic Instruction Adherence Score (IAS) for real-time floor management and an LLM-judged Persona Adherence Score (PAS) for persona-consistent content. Tests on multiple real-time speech systems including F-Actor, PersonaPlex, GPT-Realtime, MiniCPM-o, and Fun-Audio-Chat found that F-Actor and PersonaPlex showed adherence drops of 9.7% and 4.5% under persona-only conditioning compared with explicit behavioral instructions. GPT-Realtime, MiniCPM-o, and Fun-Audio-Chat maintained persona-consistent content but did not adapt floor behavior across explicit and persona-only instructions. Even when systems followed conflicting directives to their prescribed persona, they still struggled to override them under safety conflict.
💡 Why It Matters： This benchmark gives voice-agent developers a reusable test set and quantitative metrics to separate persona content generation from real-time interaction control. In customer service, companion robots, and smart home scenarios, failure to infer when to interrupt, yield, or manage overlapping speech can cause bad interruptions or missed safety constraints. The results show that role or persona configuration alone is not enough to reliably constrain real-time behavior, requiring explicit instructions, architecture changes, or conflict-resolution mechanisms.
📊 Key Facts: 9.7% 4.5% 1,038
Background
Full-duplex voice agents can listen and speak simultaneously, continuously deciding when to listen, backchannel, interrupt, handle speech overlaps, take the floor, and yield. Existing benchmarks often test these behaviors through explicit turn-management instructions, while deployed agents are commonly configured through roles or personas that only imply appropriate conversational behavior. DuplexSpeechBench-IFEval targets this implicit instruction-following gap.
Key Details
The five conditioning protocols are default behavior, explicit behavioral instructions, persona-implied behavior, combined persona-rule conditioning, and instruction conflict. IAS is deterministic while PAS is LLM-judged; architecture-dependent trade-offs appear across F-Actor, PersonaPlex, GPT-Realtime, MiniCPM-o, and Fun-Audio-Chat. Under persona-only conditioning, F-Actor and PersonaPlex dropped by 9.7% and 4.5%, whereas the other systems showed little floor-behavior adaptation but stronger persona content adherence. Safety-conflict tests indicate that inferring role-implied behavior, executing it at the right conversational moment, and resolving competing instructions remain distinct challenges.

The inference-time approach avoids retraining, allowing existing agents to be upgraded with minimal overhead.
Background

Multimodal GUI agents combine vision and language to understand screen content. Conflict-aware termination means the agent should refuse or stop when the instruction is logically inconsistent or conflicts with the current GUI state, rather than executing an action. Overcompliance refers to an AI system following instructions even when they are inappropriate or impossible. Inference-time frameworks modify model behavior during use without retraining, making them practical for deployed agents.
Key Details

GrowPage Introduces On-Demand KV Budgeting for Efficient LLM Reasoning Serving

rss ArXiv cs.AI · 2026-09-05 · ⌛ 5 min read
Reason：Novel on-demand KV cache budgeting framework targeting a critical memory bottleneck in LLM serving, with technical depth and potential practical impact; no empirical results or community feedback provided, limiting validation.
📰 What's New
GrowPage treats KV cache capacity as a runtime resource and dynamically adjusts allocation during decoding instead of relying on a predefined per-request budget. It maintains lightweight dual-timescale query summaries to capture recent and long-term attention behaviors, then uses relative attention working sets to estimate demand evolution. At each capacity boundary, GrowPage either compresses KV states within the current allocation or acquires an additional physical page when broader demand emerges. The method integrates with PagedAttention's page-level memory abstraction, preserving continuous batching and prefix caching. Experiments on reasoning benchmarks across multiple models show GrowPage achieves a superior performance-throughput trade-off over existing approaches.
💡 Why It Matters： Long-output reasoning has made the KV cache a critical memory bottleneck in LLM serving, but existing compression methods fix total capacity and cannot adapt to varying demand across requests or within a single request. GrowPage's on-demand budgeting can improve GPU memory utilization and reduce per-throughput hardware cost for inference providers. Its compatibility with continuous batching and prefix caching makes it practically deployable in production serving frameworks.
Background
The KV cache stores key and value states during autoregressive generation to avoid recomputation, and its size grows linearly with sequence length. Long-output reasoning tasks such as mathematical proofs or code generation produce many tokens, quickly filling GPU memory. PagedAttention organizes the KV cache into fixed-size physical pages, enabling flexible memory allocation and prefix sharing, and forms the foundation of inference frameworks like vLLM.
Key Details
GrowPage uses dual-timescale query summaries: one captures recent attention behavior, the other captures long-term attention behavior, and their relative attention working sets indicate whether demand is shrinking or expanding. At capacity boundaries, it can compress KV states within the current page allocation or request a new physical page. The page-level integration with PagedAttention preserves existing scheduling and memory-sharing features such as continuous batching and prefix caching.

InertiaKV Advances Aggressive KV Eviction with EMA Temporal Aggregation

rss ArXiv cs.AI · 2026-09-05 · ⌛ 5 min read
Reason：Technical paper on KV cache eviction identifies temporal aggregation as a key factor often overlooked, and introduces InertiaKV with meaningful decode throughput improvements. Relevant to AI infrastructure efficiency, but not a paradigm shift or company-level event.
📰 What's New
Researchers evaluated six open-weight backbones on LongBench, LongBench-v2, and RULER and found that under aggressive KV compression, exponential-moving-average (EMA) aggregation makes many order-preserving scorer modifications nearly indistinguishable at the eviction-set level. Value-norm and entropy variants remain highly correlated with attention and produce nearly unchanged retention sets, while KeyDiff, key norm, recency, and a learned scorer alter ranking and degrade substantially. Building on this, they introduce InertiaKV, an EMA-based decoding-time eviction method, and InertiaKV-Lazy, a periodic-refresh variant that achieves 1.34-1.46x decode throughput relative to full refresh. They also study Score-Free decoding, which scores the full context once at the first decode step, freezes ranking, and removes all subsequent scoring with an average quality change of +0.03.
💡 Why It Matters： These findings clarify that temporal aggregation and ranking preservation are distinct, consequential design factors for deployment of aggressive KV cache compression in LLM inference. The 1.34-1.46x decode throughput improvement reduces serving cost and latency for long-context workloads, while the Score-Free operating point eliminates per-step scoring overhead with negligible quality change. This can guide hardware and inference engine developers to prioritize temporal consistency over scorer complexity.
📊 Key Facts: 1.34 1.46 0.03
Background
Decoding-time KV cache eviction removes less important key-value pairs during text generation to reduce memory and computation. Scoring functions rank tokens for eviction, and temporal aggregation rules combine scores across decode steps. Exponential moving average (EMA) is a common temporal aggregation that weights recent score updates more heavily.
Key Details
The paper identifies EMA aggregation as coupling layer weighting and temporal retention, which explains why value-norm and entropy variants stay consistently correlated with attention. KeyDiff, key norm, recency, and a learned scorer break this consistency and degrade under aggressive compression. Score-Free decoding freezes the first-step ranking and achieves +0.03 average quality change while removing all subsequent scoring.
