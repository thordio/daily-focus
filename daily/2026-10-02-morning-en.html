CPU-Based Hallucination Detector Tested Across 1.5B to 120B Models

reddit r/LocalLLaMA · 2026-10-02 · ⌛ 5 min read
Reason：Technical solution for detecting hallucinations in local LLMs without GPU overhead, addressing a practical pain point for practitioners. The approach is novel and potentially useful, but not a breakthrough. No comments provided to assess discussion quality.
📰 What's New
A zero-dependency Python metric called Spanda (R_sc) detects hallucinations in local LLM responses in 1.3 microseconds on CPU with zero GPU usage. Developers benchmarked the exact-match entropy approach on GSM8K and TriviaQA across Qwen 1.5B (AUROC 0.58), Mistral 7B (0.71), Qwen 27B (0.89), and a 120B model (0.09). The 7B result matched a heavy DeBERTa NLI cross-encoder (0.706 vs 0.705), while the 27B result showed exact matching dominance with p=1.89×10⁻²⁸. On the 120B model, AUROC collapsed because the model produced identical wrong answers across all five sampling runs, a phenomenon called Confident Mode Collapse.
💡 Why It Matters： Local LLM operators running Ollama in production can now screen for hallucinations on mid-sized 7B–27B models without adding GPU overhead or 100ms+ latencies from judge models. This shifts hallucination detection from a heavy neural guardrail to a CPU-only sampling check for structured tasks like math, code, JSON extraction, and SQL. However, the 120B model's unanimous false certainty shows that larger models can defeat exact-match entropy and even NLI-based methods, so frontier deployments need different strategies.
📊 Key Facts: 1.3 0.58 0.71
Background
Semantic entropy, introduced in a Nature paper by an Oxford team, detects hallucinations by sampling multiple responses at high temperature and using a natural language inference model such as DeBERTa to group equivalent meanings before computing entropy. This approach is accurate but requires GPU memory and adds significant latency, making it impractical for consumer hardware running local models. The new method tests whether exact string matching can replace the neural clustering step, based on the observation that models with stable internal representations often output the same tokens when they know an answer.
Key Details
Spanda replaces the standard DeBERTa cross-encoder and its 45 pairwise NLI comparisons with deterministic string normalization plus Shannon entropy over exact-match clusters, cutting latency from 100ms+ to 1.3µs. The evaluation samples five responses per prompt at temperature 0.7 and uses AUROC to measure hallucination detection quality. The open-source code and multi-path generation logs are available on GitHub.

rss ArXiv cs.AI · 2026-10-02 · ⌛ 5 min read
Reason：The paper provides a controlled diagnostic showing rationales in QA pipelines may not improve answer accuracy but strongly affect verifier support judgments, with corrupted rationales causing large shifts. 📰 What's New
A message-intervention study across 400 MuSiQue, HotpotQA, and 2WikiMultiHopQA examples with DeepSeek as generator and verifier found that faithful rationales add almost no answer accuracy over no rationale, while corrupted rationales strongly alter verifier support judgments. Under a blind verifier prompt, harmless paraphrases shift support by only 0–2.5%, whereas corrupted rationales shift support by 10–22%; an explicit rationale-checking prompt amplifies the shift to 34–55%. Final answers move less (2–30%), and only 2.9–35.3% of corrupted support flips co-occur with answer changes. Human audits found 16/42 valid corruptions are corruption-overtrust cases, and blind humans reject or mark unclear 9/10 audited corrupted rationales that the model accepts.
💡 Why It Matters： For practitioners building multi-agent LLM QA systems, these results show rationale passing can introduce a new failure surface: verifiers may accept corrupted rationales that humans would reject, skewing support judgments without improving answer accuracy. This suggests rationale sharing should be evaluated as a verification-message mechanism with explicit checks, rather than as a default route to higher accuracy. Cross-model and task-boundary checks reveal conditions where the channel is active, amplified, inert, or folded into the task label, informing when to include rationales.
📊 Key Facts: 2.5% 22% 55%
Background
Role-specialized QA systems divide tasks between a reasoner that produces explanations and a verifier that checks answers and support; rationales are natural-language explanations passed between them. Multi-hop QA datasets like MuSiQue, HotpotQA, and 2WikiMultiHopQA require combining information from multiple documents to answer a question. Prior work often assumes rationales improve answer accuracy or auditability, but this study tests whether the rationale itself communicates signal to the verifier.
Key Details
The diagnostic fixes evidence and candidate answer and only varies the rationale passed from reasoner to verifier, isolating the message effect. Using DeepSeek as generator and verifier on 400 multi-hop QA examples, the study compares blind verifier prompts versus explicit rationale-checking prompts, with the latter amplifying support shifts to 34–55%. Human audits define corruption-overtrust: 16 of 42 valid corruptions were accepted by the model but rejected by blind humans, and 9 of 10 audited corrupted rationales were rejected or marked unclear by humans.

Personalization aims to align a model's outputs with an individual user's style or utility rather than aggregate human preferences. Standard DPO often selects training pairs by likelihood-based heuristics, such as choosing high- and low-probability completions, which may not reflect the direction that improves a specific user's utility.
Key Details

rss ArXiv cs.AI · 2026-10-02 · ⌛ 5 min read
Reason：Novel arXiv research with practical implications for LLM training data curation; demonstrates that transfer follows reasoning approach more than topic, which could influence data selection strategies. No community discussion provided.
📰 What's New
Researchers fine-tuned five base models across two counterbalanced 2×2 experimental designs to compare transfer from training examples that share a reasoning approach versus a topic. In the primary design covering probability and combinatorics with invariant reasoning and double counting across 2,000 problems, same-approach sources outperformed same-topic sources by 8.2 to 16.2 percentage points (mean 10.8). In a second design with number theory and geometry using complement and pigeonhole reasoning over 800 problems, the same-approach advantage was 12.0 to 16.0 percentage points (mean 14.3). All 40 seed-pooled model–target comparisons favored same-approach transfer, and all ten model-level 95% confidence intervals excluded zero.
💡 Why It Matters： These results suggest that curating math training data by solution method rather than by topic could improve LLM fine-tuning outcomes, potentially reducing data acquisition costs and improving performance on out-of-domain problems. Practitioners building specialized math models may need to label or cluster examples by reasoning approach, not just by domain. The finding also challenges resemblance-based data selection, since same-topic examples were more similar under embedding and lexical measures but produced worse transfer.
📊 Key Facts: 95% 2,000 8.2
Background
Transfer learning in LLMs refers to how skills learned on training examples improve performance on held-out tasks. Fine-tuning adapts a pretrained model to a narrower task using additional examples. In mathematics, topics are domains like probability or number theory, while a reasoning approach is the method used to solve a problem, such as double counting or pigeonhole reasoning. The study isolates whether transfer follows shared method or shared subject matter.
Key Details
The study used a counterbalanced design where every source appears once in same-approach and once in same-topic roles, so additive source-quality effects cancel from the equally weighted aggregate contrast. The four reasoning methods tested were invariant reasoning, double counting, complement reasoning, and pigeonhole reasoning. Same-topic sources were more similar to targets under embedding and lexical measures, yet same-approach transfer won, indicating statement-level resemblance does not predict mathematical transfer.

Study Separates Memory Retention from Retrieval in Agent Evaluation

rss ArXiv cs.AI · 2026-10-02 · ⌛ 5 min read
Reason：Technical paper with methodological value for AI agent memory evaluation: it disentangles retention from retrieval, identifies confounding in prior comparisons, and quantifies effects. High relevance for AI practitioners building agents, but not a market-moving event. No community discussion available.
📰 What's New
A streaming-recall benchmark crossed retention and selection rules and evaluated every condition on the same 300 seeded episodes. Holding access fixed, query-aware selection improved required-fact recall by 15.5 percentage points (95% CI: 12.8–18.2), while a mixed comparison changing history access reported a 68.7-point advantage, with 53.2 points attributable to access alone. Under bounded retention, query-aware, dense, and oracle selection all reached the retention ceiling, and all 319 observed failures in the bounded recency condition came from eviction rather than ranking errors. Recall fell to 0% once targets receded sufficiently far into the past, and a repeat on SQuAD preserved the retention ceiling while dense retrieval outperformed lexical retrieval on natural text.
💡 Why It Matters： The result provides a methodological correction for AI agent memory evaluation: comparisons must hold history access fixed, otherwise access differences inflate apparent gains by as much as 53.2 points. Practitioners building bounded-memory agents can now distinguish whether failures come from not storing a fact or not surfacing it, which directs effort toward eviction policies versus ranking. The SQuAD replication suggests the retention-ceiling finding generalizes beyond synthetic episodes.
📊 Key Facts: 95% 0% 15.5
Background
In bounded-memory agents, retention decides which incoming information to store within a limited buffer, while retrieval or selection decides which stored items to surface for a query. Evaluations that compare methods differing in both dimensions can confound the two causes of failure, as this study shows. Streaming-recall benchmarks test agents on episodes where information arrives sequentially and queries arrive later, mimicking real-world assistants.
Key Details
The benchmark crosses retention and selection rules to isolate their effects rather than using monolithic memory systems. The bounded recency condition produced 319 failures, all of which were due to eviction, meaning retrieval ranking made no contribution to those errors. Query-aware selection improved recall by 15.5 points when access was held fixed, compared with a 68.7-point mixed comparison effect.
