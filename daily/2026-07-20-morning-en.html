Paper Proposes Independent AI Certification to Close Trust Gap

rss ArXiv cs.AI · 2026-07-20 · ⌛ 4 min read
Reason：Addresses a critical structural issue in AI market signaling with strong potential to influence industry standards, regulation, and investor perception of AI trustworthiness.
📰 What's New
A new paper from the Digital Trust Council identifies a structural 'trust gap': responsible AI practices exist internally, but no market signal shows whether systems are truly trustworthy. The paper attributes this to three compounding failures: (1) the market cannot distinguish trustworthy systems from imitations; (2) evaluation targets models, not deployed sociotechnical outcomes; (3) the measurement ecosystem avoids demonstrating benefits, focusing only on harm mitigation. It proposes independent, outcome-oriented certification modeled on healthcare, sustainability, and security regimes.
💡 Why It Matters： The proposal addresses a critical barrier to AI adoption: firms investing in safety cannot prove it. Independent certification could create market incentives for trustworthiness, akin to LEED or Fair Trade labels, influencing procurement, regulation, and investor decisions.
Background
Responsible AI (RAI) includes bias audits, transparency efforts, and risk management. However, these remain internal and non-standardized. Certification regimes in other sectors, such as ISO standards or Fair Trade, provide independent verification and market differentiation. AI currently lacks such a mechanism, creating a trust deficit.
Key Details
The paper distinguishes 'responsible AI' (internal process) from 'trustworthy AI' (verifiable outcomes). It reviews existing instruments like conformity assessment and attestation, finding none combine governance baseline, positive-outcome evidence, and market signaling. The proposed certification layer complements regulation.

From Black Box to Executable Logic: Explainable Reinforcement Learning through Prolog Expert Systems

rss ArXiv cs.AI · 2026-07-20 · ⌛ 5 min read
Reason：Presents a novel method for converting deep RL policies into explainable Prolog programs with formal performance guarantees, representing important progress in explainable AI, though not a paradigm-shifting breakthrough.
📰 What's New
A three-stage post-hoc method distills a frozen PPO policy into an ordered rule list and emits it as an executable Prolog program. An expansion stage edits the rule base, accepting changes only when policy evaluation certifies a return increase. Four theoretical guarantees are proven: a return-loss bound makes the distilled program a machine-checkable certificate in finite MDPs, and the expansion loop improves monotonically and terminates. Empirically, on a two-room key-and-door task with 16,944 reachable states, the expanded Prolog program achieves exact optimal return in every seed; on Acrobot, it matches the neural teacher with just 11 clauses, recovers about 97% of the return on CartPole, and partially recovers on LunarLander as predicted by the exponential lower bound.
💡 Why It Matters： This transformation makes deep RL policies human-readable, verifiable, and editable, addressing the critical need for interpretability in safety-critical applications like autonomous driving and medical diagnosis. It bridges symbolic AI and deep learning, allowing domain experts to inject knowledge without retraining neural networks. The formal return guarantees provide a reliability foundation for deploying such systems, potentially accelerating approval in regulated industries.
📊 Key Facts: 97% 16,944
Background
Prolog is a logic programming language based on first-order logic, widely used for symbolic reasoning and building expert systems. Expert systems represent knowledge as “if-then” rules, mimicking human expert decision processes. Explainable AI (XAI) seeks to make machine learning models transparent to humans. Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) is a popular reinforcement learning algorithm, and a Markov Decision Process (MDP) is the standard mathematical framework for sequential decision-making. This work is the first to convert a deep RL policy into an editable Prolog program with formal performance guarantees.
Key Details
The method proceeds in three stages: extracting decisions from a frozen PPO teacher, inducing an ordered rule list via classical relational learning, and outputting a Prolog program executed by an off-the-shelf logic engine. For continuous observations, propositional threshold instantiation converts the network with fidelity O(1/B) as resolution B grows, but the cost is exponential in observation dimension for oblique decision boundaries. The expansion loop uses policy evaluation to ensure monotonic return improvement and guaranteed termination in finite MDPs.
