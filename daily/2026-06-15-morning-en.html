Representational Geometry Predicts LLM Compositional Errors

rss ArXiv cs.AI · 2026-06-15 · ⌛ 3 min read
Reason：This paper introduces a novel approach to anticipate LLM compositional failures by analyzing representational geometry, which is highly valuable for AI researchers and practitioners aiming to improve model reliability without exhaustive benchmarking. While not a market-moving breakthrough, it addresses a critical challenge in systematic composition and provides foundational insights for future work.
📰 What's New
A new paper on arXiv introduces Adversarial Concept Search, a method that uses an LLM's internal representational geometry to predict compositional errors. The researchers validated the method on toy programmatic tasks, multihop reasoning, and multilingual factual recall. They found that when concepts are encoded near-orthogonally, the model composes them reliably; when linear encodings are closely aligned, causing interference, the model fails. The method anticipates failure modes without evaluating specific inputs.
💡 Why It Matters： This approach enables developers to identify high-risk concept combinations upfront, reducing the need for exhaustive benchmarking. It offers a scalable foundation for active learning and targeted stress testing, potentially improving the reliability of LLMs in combinatorial tasks.
Background
In LLMs, concepts are represented as vectors in high-dimensional space. Compositional errors happen when models fail to combine simple concepts into complex ones. Representational geometry studies the organization of these vectors; orthogonality between encodings reduces interference and enables reliable composition.
Key Details
The method analyzes the alignment of linear encoding directions for atomic concepts. Near-orthogonality indicates independent composition, while closely aligned directions produce interference and lead to failure. No evaluation of specific input instances is required.

Background
