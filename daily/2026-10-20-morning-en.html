Self-Improving Video-to-Action Models Without Action Data or Rewards

rss ArXiv cs.AI · 2026-10-20 · ⌛ 5 min read
Reason：Introduces Mirror, a novel self-supervised method for learning video-to-action mappings without action data or rewards, addressing a key scalability bottleneck in embodied AI. Shows strong empirical results across multiple benchmarks, indicating important progress with potential impact on robotics and video-based planning.
📰 What's New
Researchers introduced Mirror, a self-supervised approach that learns video-to-action mappings through environment interactions, requiring no prior action data or hand-crafted rewards. Mirror operates by attempting to follow a visual plan—executing its best guess of the underlying actions—and then trains on the resulting video-action pairs, incrementally self-improving. On Atari, OGBench, and DeepMind Control, Mirror outperforms leading learning-from-observation methods when using learned video plans, and achieves near-expert performance with oracle plans. In Minecraft BASALT, it beats methods that have access to expert actions or hand-engineered task knowledge. In Minecraft survival, Mirror is the first method to acquire an iron pickaxe through self-improvement alone, without rewards or expert action labels.
💡 Why It Matters： By removing the need for large human-collected video-action datasets and hand-crafted rewards, Mirror lowers the data and engineering barrier for deploying embodied agents. This means advances in video generative models could directly translate into more capable robots and planners, with particular impact on scalable visual planning and learning from observation.
Background
Video generative models can act as visual planners by producing a video of task completion, but moving from video to executable actions typically requires an inverse dynamics model trained on large video-action datasets. Learning from observation is a family of methods that attempt to learn policies from videos alone, but many still rely on hand-designed rewards or expert data, limiting scalability. Mirror addresses this by treating environment interaction as a self-supervision signal: the agent attempts to realize the plan, observes the outcome, and improves.
Key Details
Mirror generates its own training data by executing guessed actions in the environment and pairing them with the resulting videos, enabling incremental self-improvement. The evaluations cover Atari, OGBench, DeepMind Control, and Minecraft BASALT; in offline settings with expert action labels but no environment interaction, it sets a new state of the art in obtaining diamonds. The method is self-supervised, requiring neither expert action labels nor reward functions during interactive learning.

Adding cross-sectional fundamental ranks raised mean effective-date RankIC by 0.0089 and following-day RankIC by 0.0098 relative to disclosure alone, while analyst-summary context provided no comparable average gain. Finance-specialized models showed an effective-event-date group advantage but no statistically reliable following-day advantage; general-purpose Qwen models produced the strongest individual return-ranking results. 💡 Why It Matters： The evaluation establishes a quantitative benchmark for extracting market-relevant signals from SEC filings with LLMs, directly informing asset managers, quantitative funds, and fintech platforms deciding whether to deploy such models. The findings show that textual analysis alone underperforms cross-sectional fundamental comparisons, and that direct LLM forecasts rarely beat individual analysts, imposing concrete limits on AI-driven investment research. Consensus anchoring improves forecast alignment with analyst ranges but may reduce EPS accuracy, forcing designers to trade off coverage and error.
📊 Key Facts: 41.1% 59.5% 45,964
Background
A Form 10-Q is a quarterly report filed with the SEC, and its MD&A section contains management's discussion of operations, liquidity, and forward-looking risks. RankIC is a rank correlation metric used in quantitative finance to assess whether a model's scores order assets in line with realized returns. The analyst distribution refers to the range of sell-side analysts' earnings per share forecasts, often used as a measure of consensus and uncertainty.

EPS forecasting was tested under direct and consensus-anchored interfaces, with consensus anchoring shifting forecasts toward analyst agreement. The 45,964 filing events span 2001–2024 Form 10-Q MD&A sections, and Qwen models outperformed other general-purpose LLMs on effective-date return ranking.

Reason：Presents a systematic analysis of context-to-LoRA methods and introduces Cache2LoRA, which improves fact retention and instruction following across 17 tasks; a solid technical contribution for efficient LLM context handling, though pre-print and no community discussion to validate impact.
📰 What's New
Researchers introduce Cache2LoRA, a query-conditioned method that aggregates evidence from a reusable frozen document cache before generating LoRA updates. Across 17 tasks, it achieves a macro F1 of 0.663, approaching full-context in-context learning at 0.671. For 32K documents and 256-token outputs at batch size 16, its decoding throughput is 5.9× that of full-context ICL. At matched memory size, generated LoRA preserves answer accuracy more reliably than learned-KV variants under long, diverse intervening text.
💡 Why It Matters： For LLM serving systems that must process long documents, Cache2LoRA’s 5.9× decoding throughput and 0.663 macro F1 suggest a practical path to reduce KV cache memory while preserving near full-context accuracy. This could lower deployment costs for retrieval-augmented generation and document QA at scale. The result also addresses a known context-to-LoRA weakness: recovered facts may not follow instructions.
📊 Key Facts: 0.663 0.671 5.9
Background
Low-rank adaptation (LoRA) injects trainable low-rank matrices to adjust model behavior without full fine-tuning. Context-to-LoRA methods compress an input document into such an update, so the model does not need to keep the full document in its limited attention context or KV cache. This matters for long-document processing because retaining all source text during decoding increases memory and slows generation.
Key Details
Cache2LoRA uses each query to aggregate evidence from a reusable frozen document cache before generating LoRA updates; trainable encoding operates only on the query and memory-token suffix. Multi-style supervision teaches both evidence preservation and answer expression. At matched memory size, combining generated LoRA with retained document KV yields higher hybrid F1 and exact match.

Transformers Forget Prompts at Power-Law Rate, Conditional No-Hair Theorem Shows

rss ArXiv cs.AI · 2026-10-20 · ⌛ 5 min read
Reason：Novel theoretical result characterizing prompt forgetting in transformers with empirical validation and a proven bound; valuable for researchers working on long-context and prompt design, but limited immediate market or industry impact. No discussion comments provided.
📰 What's New
A study posted as arXiv:2610.24133 reports measurements of how autoregressive transformers retain their prompts after generating t tokens: given different prompts followed by the same continuation text, the distance between hidden states decays approximately as a power law t^{-α}. Trained models forget fast (α≈0.55–0.7), untrained ones slowly (α≈0.2–0.5). The paper proves an offset bound O(P(log t)^{L-1}/t) for prompt length P and depth L, valid for finite-depth pre-LN softmax transformers with bounded attention logits and pre-norm activations away from zero. A solvable linear relay model shows the bound cannot be improved and reproduces the rise of the local exponent toward 1 seen in untrained networks.
💡 Why It Matters： For researchers and engineers building long-context models or designing prompts, the result quantifies a hard limit: prompt-only influence on the next token vanishes as t grows, so any surviving property such as language or instruction adherence must be carried by the generated text itself. This provides a theoretical basis for observed failures in long generations and suggests that prompt design needs to encode constraints into generated content or use bias compensation rather than relying on the prompt alone.
📊 Key Facts: 0.55 0.7 0.2
Background
In autoregressive transformers, hidden states are internal vector representations updated token by token, and prompts are the initial tokens that set context. The term "no-hair theorem" is borrowed from black hole physics, where external properties disappear except conserved quantities; analogously, prompt properties disappear from hidden-state influence except those transferred to the generated text. The power law t^{-α} describes the rate at which prompt influence fades, with larger α meaning faster forgetting.
Key Details
The mechanism is geometric: the prompt's direct influence is diluted as context grows, and relay—continuation tokens passing on what earlier tokens read from the prompt—only adds logarithmic factors. The exactly solvable linear relay model proves the bound is tight and reproduces the rise of the local exponent toward 1 seen in untrained networks; adding a logit bias to cancel dilution stops decay early in training and reverses it afterward, showing dilution is necessary for the measured forgetting and sufficient in untrained networks and the solvable model.

Researchers introduced Resonance, a set of open-weight models for forward chemical reaction prediction trained by distilling reasoning traces from a frontier open-weight teacher and then self-training on verified outputs. 💡 Why It Matters： For pharmaceutical and chemistry teams that need open, adaptable models for synthesis planning, distillation and self-training can turn inaccessible frontier models into deployable predictors. Distillation trains a smaller student model to mimic a larger teacher's outputs or reasoning traces, while self-training uses a model's own confident predictions as additional labels. Pushback testing challenges a model's answer to see if it changes under doubt, revealing whether the model has robust reasoning or merely pattern-matching.
Key Details

LIFT: Training-Free Prompt Selection for Efficient RLVR Using Partial Rollouts

rss ArXiv cs.AI · 2026-10-20 · ⌛ 6 min read
Reason：Introduces a practical method for reducing RLVR training cost via training-free prompt selection using partial rollouts; relevant to AI researchers and practitioners but not a paradigm shift. No discussion comments available.
📰 What's New
LIFT is a training-free data selector that uses partial reasoning rollouts to identify impactful prompts for reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR). Its core, Partial Rollout Context Lift (PRCL), is inspired by pointwise V-information and accumulates token-level log-likelihood ratios between a question-conditioned context and a null context with the question removed. On Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B and DeepSeek-R1-Distill-1.5B, prefixes retaining at least 90% and 95% of the full-rollout PRCL score require only 26% and 4.7% of the tokens generated by full rollouts. Compared with SHIFT, LIFT reduces selection cost by 3.7–9.1× while achieving competitive downstream RLVR performance, with the highest six-benchmark average in three of six settings.
💡 Why It Matters： RLVR is compute-intensive, making training-data selection a practical lever for reducing cost. LIFT offers a training-free alternative that scores prompts from short prefixes instead of full trajectories, cutting selection token usage to 26% or 4.7% in the reported cases. This matters for AI labs and practitioners training reasoning models such as Qwen2.5-Math and DeepSeek-R1-Distill on verifiable-reward tasks, where rollout generation dominates expense.
📊 Key Facts: 90% 95% 26%
Background
Reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) trains reasoning models by rewarding them when a final answer can be automatically checked, such as a math solution. Unlike supervised fine-tuning, RLVR requires generating rollouts for each prompt, which is expensive. Prompt selection methods try to choose a small subset of training prompts that contribute the most to learning. Pointwise V-information is an information-theoretic measure of how much usable information one variable provides about another; PRCL adapts this idea to compare question-conditioned and question-removed contexts. SHIFT is a prior training-free selection method that relies on full hidden-state trajectories, whereas LIFT uses partial rollouts.
Key Details
PRCL accumulates token-level log-likelihood ratios between a question-conditioned context and a null context with the question removed, enabling prompt scoring from a short rollout prefix rather than a complete trajectory. The authors report that 26–32% of complete greedy rollouts fail to terminate within the generation budget in their datasets, motivating the partial-rollout approach. On Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B and DeepSeek-R1-Distill-1.5B, LIFT's selected prefixes retain at least 90% and 95% of full-rollout PRCL while using 26% and 4.7% of generated tokens.
