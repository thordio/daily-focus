📰 What's New
Researchers introduced KV-PRM, a process reward model that eliminates text re-encoding by directly reading the KV cache produced during LLM generation. By processing a single 'verify token' against the pre-existing KV cache, it reduces scoring cost from O(L²) to O(L). On MATH, GSM8K, and AIME benchmarks, KV-PRM matches or outperforms text-PRMs under test-time scaling methods like beam search, MCTS, and weighted voting, achieving up to a 5,000× reduction in scoring FLOPs, 37× latency reduction, and 34× memory reduction per sequence.
💡 Why It Matters： This addresses a critical bottleneck in long-horizon multi-agent systems, enabling test-time scaling to be applied efficiently to complex reasoning tasks. By making process reward models scalable to long sequences, it could accelerate progress in automated problem-solving, code generation, and scientific discovery where LLM agents operate over extended interactions.
📊 Key Facts: 5,000
Background
Process reward models (PRMs) evaluate intermediate steps during reasoning to guide search algorithms. Traditional PRMs re-encode the full text at each scoring step, leading to quadratic costs as sequence length grows. KV cache is the internal key-value state used in transformer models for efficient autoregressive generation. Test-time scaling (TTS) refers to strategies that improve performance by allocating extra compute during inference, such as majority voting, beam search, and Monte Carlo tree search.
Key Details
KV-PRM bypasses text re-encoding by leveraging the existing KV cache, using a single verify token to query the entire history. The paper formally proves that KV cache contains strictly greater information capacity than text for reward modeling. Experiments cover three math benchmarks and three TTS strategies, with consistent superiority over text-PRMs.

Reason：Novel method enabling controllable molecular generation via flow matching without extra computation; high practical value for drug discovery and AI-driven design.
📰 What's New
A new method, Reward Transport, uses optimal transport coupling during flow matching training to align a scalar noise-space coordinate with molecular reward values. At inference, varying this coordinate continuously steers generated molecular properties without requiring a separate reward model or gradient guidance. On the ZINC-250K and GuacaMol datasets, sweeping the coordinate induces monotonic control of logP and consistent control of QED across the operating range. The same control knob produces opposite structural responses: it grows molecules to increase logP but shrinks them to increase QED, ruling out a generic size bias.
💡 Why It Matters： This method enables continuous property control in molecular generation without additional inference cost, significantly benefiting molecular optimization for drug discovery. It complements classifier-free guidance and conditional flow matching, and the negative result in epsilon-prediction diffusion reveals structural absence of coupling-level alignment, guiding future generative model design.
Background
Flow matching is a generative model that learns a velocity field transforming noise samples into data samples. Optimal transport coupling determines how noise vectors are paired with data points during training, traditionally treated as a computational choice. This work repurposes it as an alignment interface, using property values to guide pairing and embed control directly into noise space.
Key Details
Training uses property-aligned optimal transport coupling to pair noise vectors with data points, embedding molecular rewards into a scalar noise coordinate. Varying this coordinate at inference, in the coupling-preserving limit, recovers the truncated reward distribution of the Cross-Entropy Method, providing a continuously adjustable distribution-level control knob. The method is implemented in a flow matching framework and open-sourced at https://github.com/KehanGuo2/reward-transport.

📰 What's New
Flash-MSA is introduced as a technique that employs sparse attention kernels to accelerate the training of models on sequences of up to one million tokens. The method reduces both memory footprint and computational cost by selectively attending only to relevant token pairs, avoiding the quadratic blowup of full attention. This enables practical training of long-context models without requiring exorbitant hardware resources. The kernels are optimized to leverage modern GPU architectures for maximal efficiency.
💡 Why It Matters： Training on million-token contexts unlocks applications like full-book summarization, repository-level code understanding, and long-term conversational agents. By drastically cutting training time and resource consumption, Flash-MSA democratizes access to long-context research, allowing smaller labs to experiment with models that previously demanded industrial-scale clusters. This could accelerate progress in fields that hinge on long-range dependencies, such as legal document analysis and scientific literature mining.
Background
Standard self-attention in Transformers has quadratic time and memory complexity with respect to sequence length, making training on sequences beyond a few thousand tokens prohibitive. Sparse attention alleviates this by allowing each token to attend to only a subset of others, based on patterns like locality or learned sparsity. In million-token training, even sparse patterns must be implemented via highly optimized custom kernels to avoid overhead and fully utilize hardware parallelism. Previous works like Longformer and BigBird already demonstrated the viability of sparse attention, but specialized training kernels for million-scale contexts remained an open challenge.
Key Details
Flash-MSA likely builds on IO-aware algorithms like FlashAttention, incorporating structured sparsity patterns such as sliding window, dilated window, or global token attention. The sparse kernel reduces the attention computation complexity from O(L²) to O(L·k), where k is a constant window size or a dynamically selected subset. The implementation probably fuses memory operations to minimize data movement between GPU high-bandwidth memory and streaming multiprocessors, a critical factor for long sequences.

Lattice Traversal Reduces MLP Adversarial Robustness to Interval Certifications

rss ArXiv cs.AI · 2026-07-13 · ⌛ 4 min read
Reason：Presents a novel theoretical framework for adversarial robustness in MLPs, introducing complete interval certifications (previously unexamined) and reducing the problem to lattice traversal. Of high value to AI researchers working on formal verification and AI safety, though immediate market impact is limited.
📰 What's New
A new theoretical framework reduces adversarial robustness certification for multilayered perceptrons (MLPs) to a lattice traversal problem. It introduces complete interval certifications, a previously unexamined concept where leaving a certified interval guarantees a prediction change. The framework includes lattice traversal operators applied in an iterative refine-and-verify scheme, achieving sound maximality and complete minimality. For complete certifications, the minimum solution is found in polynomial oracle calls, while sound certifications show strong intractability.
💡 Why It Matters： This work brings formal guarantees to AI safety by rigorously defining both sound and complete certifications. The findings on tractability for complete certifications and intractability for sound certifications inform the feasibility of formal verification tools. The approach could impact adversarial robustness testing in safety-critical MLP applications.
Background
Adversarial robustness ensures that small perturbations to an MLP's input do not alter its prediction, a cornerstone of AI safety. Interval certification verifies robustness over a hyper-rectangle region rather than just a single point. Existing work focused on sound certifications (no false negatives), but complete certifications (guaranteeing detection of all vulnerable perturbations) were unexplored due to computational challenges.
Key Details
The lattice traversal operators guide an iterative process that refines interval certifications until sound maximality and complete minimality are guaranteed. Objective optimization reveals that complete minimal certifications are polynomially solvable, whereas sound maximality is strongly intractable. For symmetric intervals (ℓ∞-spheres), logarithmic algorithms are provided, and the system is implemented in the ParallelepipedoNN tool.
