Conserved Verdicts and Drifting Commitments Identify Harmful Overthinking in Reasoning Models

rss ArXiv cs.AI · 2026-11-18 · ⌛ 5 min read
Reason：Novel technical analysis of overthinking in reasoning models, introducing commitment-verdict gap and empirical measurement on MATH-500; highly relevant to AI researchers and practitioners optimizing reasoning model efficiency and reliability, though no community discussion provided.
📰 What's New
On MATH-500, at all 379 points where DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B held a correct answer, continuing to reason lost 3.6 percentage points of accuracy on average (95% CI [2.2, 5.2]), with losses concentrated in a few problems. The analysis introduces commitment and verdict probabilities to compare answer-if-stopped-now correctness with final-answer correctness at the same chain position. It shows that verdict alone cannot guide stopping because, as a forecast of a fixed outcome, it has no predictable drift. The commitment-verdict gap equals the expected remaining change in answer quality and the accuracy gained by stopping.
💡 Why It Matters： For teams deploying reasoning models, overthinking wastes tokens and can reduce accuracy; the new framework identifies what to measure before building stopping rules. An oracle stopping rule gains at most +1.8 percentage points at 1.5B scale and +2.5 at 7B, indicating early stopping alone has limited upside. The decomposition gives model providers and researchers a precise way to distinguish harmful continuation from survivor bias in accuracy-budget curves.
📊 Key Facts: 95% 3.6 2.2
Background
Reasoning models produce intermediate chain-of-thought tokens before a final answer; overthinking occurs when continuing after a correct answer changes it to incorrect. MATH-500 is a benchmark of 500 competition-level mathematics problems, and DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B is a 1.5-billion-parameter reasoning model distilled from DeepSeek-R1. Final-correctness probes typically estimate only the probability that the final answer is correct, which this work argues is insufficient for stopping decisions.
Key Details
The commitment probability is defined through a specified answer-if-stopped-now operator; its gap from the conserved verdict equals both expected remaining drift and conditional stopping accuracy gain. A second identity separates accuracy-versus-budget changes into within-chain answer change and changes in which chains are still running. Across six models from three families on MATH-500 and OlympiadBench with budgets up to 16,384 tokens, declining accuracy curves were driven by which chains remained, while surviving chains kept or improved answers.

Long-Context Generation Is a Sampling Problem: Top-nsigma Wins at High Temperature

rss ArXiv cs.AI · 2026-11-18 · ⌛ 4 min read
Reason：Important technical finding showing that sampler choice, not additional training, determines long-context generation quality at high temperatures, with dramatic performance differences on SWE-bench coding tasks; no community discussion provided.
📰 What's New
Researchers tested 29 open-weight models from 1B to 122B parameters across ten benchmarks and needle retrieval up to one million tokens. At temperature 2, coding agents using top-p, top-k, or Top-H solved 0 of 100 GitHub issues on SWE-bench Verified, while the same agents using top-nsigma solved 46 to 62 and won every disagreement on three Qwen3.5 models. Across all benchmarks, top-nsigma kept a median 100% of its temperature-1 score, compared to 26% for top-p.
💡 Why It Matters： This shifts the fix for degraded long-context generation from additional training to sampler configuration, reducing cost and complexity for open-weight model deployments. Agentic coding and long-horizon tasks at elevated temperatures can retain accuracy simply by switching sampling strategies.
📊 Key Facts: 100% 26% 3.5
Background
top-p samples from the smallest set of tokens whose cumulative probability reaches p; top-k samples from the k most likely tokens; top-nsigma selects tokens within a number of standard deviations of the probability distribution. Temperature scaling flattens the distribution, making low-probability tokens more likely. In long-context generation, allowing too many low-probability tokens leads to incoherent or repetitive output.
Key Details
The token pool explains the gap: in a Llama-3.1-8B story, raising temperature from 1 to 2 grew top-p's candidate pool from 4 to 51,845 tokens, while top-nsigma stayed at 3. Across 133 sampler-temperature combinations, typical pool size predicted judged quality with a Spearman correlation of -0.77. Selecting the pool at temperature 1 and applying temperature afterwards stopped Top-H, min-p, and p-less from collapsing.

Attribution-Guided Pruning Is Fragile for Reasoning Language Models

rss ArXiv cs.AI · 2026-11-18 · ⌛ 5 min read
Reason：This arXiv paper provides high-value technical analysis showing that attribution-guided pruning for reasoning language models is unreliable, with significant accuracy degradation when using final-answer token scoring. It directly impacts AI practitioners working on model compression and offers a cautionary insight into an increasingly used technique.
📰 What's New
Researchers evaluated attribution-guided pruning across four small reasoning models and found that scoring final-answer tokens saturates, with answer-token negative log-likelihood between 1e-4 and 1e-3 in every chain-of-thought model tested. Guiding head pruning with this target costs 27.6 and 32.2 accuracy points relative to scoring reasoning tokens on two models across five seeds, while perplexity barely moves and even reverses sign at 7B. Single-neuron causal effects sit at the measurement floor, cross-split rankings are noise, and group ablation effects disagree in sign with summed member effects in 90% of groups; reproducible importance emerges only at groups of about 100 neurons. The pre-registered hypothesis that distillation-induced concentration of importance predicts pruning cost was not supported, and was reported unresolved rather than reversed due to confounding.
💡 Why It Matters： For AI practitioners compressing reasoning language models, these results imply that attribution-guided pruning based on final-answer tokens can silently introduce 27–32 point accuracy losses that perplexity does not reveal. The findings suggest switching to reasoning-token scoring, coarse-grained attribution, and accuracy-based evaluation, which would change how model compression is validated.
📊 Key Facts: 90% 27.6 32.2
Background
Attribution-guided pruning removes neural network components with the smallest estimated causal contribution; it is increasingly proposed to compress reasoning language models without destroying chain-of-thought behavior. Reasoning models emit intermediate reasoning tokens before their final answer, so pruning methods must choose which token positions to score. Scoring final-answer tokens is a natural choice but saturates because the model states its answer within its own reasoning, making causal importance difficult to measure.
Key Details
The study tested four small reasoning models, used five seeds for head pruning, and found answer-token negative log-likelihood saturates in all chain-of-thought models at values between 1e-4 and 1e-3. Single-neuron rankings are noise across splits, 90% of group ablation effects disagree in sign with summed member effects, and stable importance requires groups of roughly 100 neurons. Contrastively isolated reasoning-specific importance is not separable from generic importance, and protecting it performs worse than protecting raw importance.

The benchmark uses controlled factual conflicts in Harvey LAB document-review tasks to measure retrieval and detection rates.
Key Details
