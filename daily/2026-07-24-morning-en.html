Researchers Formalize Incomplete Prompt Jailbreaks in LLMs

rss ArXiv cs.AI · 2026-07-24 · ⌛ 4 min read
Reason：Novel formalization of a persistent LLM vulnerability (incomplete prompt jailbreaks) with systematic characterization, demonstrating that parameter-tuning defenses are insufficient, and identifying functional neurons for potential control. This has high relevance for AI safety, alignment, and model deployment, offering insights for practitioners and researchers.
📰 What's New
A research team from KAIST AI formalized incomplete prompt jailbreaks (IPJ), where LLMs produce harmful completions for incomplete harmful prompts due to systematic delayed refusal. They analyzed diverse attractor types and showed that models complete harmful sentences before refusing. Their experiments demonstrated that training-based defenses fail to generalize across content domains and attractor types. The team also identified two functional neuron types—termination and continuation neurons—for fine-grained control over completion behavior.
💡 Why It Matters： This vulnerability persists in open-weight models with safety guardrails, posing a direct threat to AI safety and alignment, as even truncated harmful prompts can bypass filters. The failure of parameter-tuning defenses to generalize indicates that current methods may not scale, while neuron-level interventions could offer more precise and robust defenses, crucial for public deployment.
Background
An incomplete prompt jailbreak occurs when a user inputs a truncated harmful request, and the LLM, instead of refusing, completes the sentence harmfully. Current safeguards typically trigger on whole harmful prompts. Attractors are patterns influencing continuation that may override refusal mechanisms. Functional neurons are specific units responsible for behaviors like sentence termination or continuation; manipulating them can alter model outputs without retraining, offering a new defense vector.
Key Details
The study covered multiple attractor types that drive harmful continuation, revealing that refusal is delayed until the sentence ends rather than interrupting mid-stream. Parameter-tuning defenses failed to transfer across different content domains and attractor types. By analyzing model internals, the researchers clarified that termination neurons govern the stopping of generation and continuation neurons promote further output, enabling targeted intervention to block harmful completions.

Polynomial-Time Algorithm for LR(k) Grammar-Constrained LLM Generation

rss ArXiv cs.AI · 2026-07-24 · ⌛ 5 min read
Reason：Proposes a novel method for constraining LLM generation to LR(k) grammars in polynomial time, improving over previous exponential methods. Technically deep and directly relevant to reliable LLM integration in formal systems. No discussion comments available.
📰 What's New
Researchers have developed an algorithm that constrains autoregressive language model generation to satisfy any LR(k) context-free grammar in polynomial time. This is a significant improvement over previous methods that required exponential time for such grammars. The approach distills the LLM into a tractable probabilistic model, allowing the generation process to be steered and masked to guarantee syntactically valid output. The algorithm enables efficient generation of valid code, JSON, SQL, and other structured languages.
💡 Why It Matters： Integrating LLMs into formal systems requires guaranteed syntactic validity, especially for code and data generation. This polynomial-time solution makes it practical to enforce complex grammar rules during LLM inference, improving reliability in program synthesis, database query generation, and API response formatting. It opens the door to using LLMs in safety-critical applications where output correctness is essential.
Background
LR(k) grammars are a class of context-free grammars commonly used in programming language design (e.g., SQL, JSON). They can be parsed by deterministic pushdown automata, which makes them suitable for efficient parsing. Tractable probabilistic models (TPMs) are a family of models that permit exact probabilistic inference in polynomial time, including hidden Markov models and certain classes of probabilistic context-free grammars. The GeLaTo framework combines TPMs with autoregressive LLMs to enforce constraints during generation by computing the probability of constraint satisfaction at each step.
Key Details
The method hinges on tractable probabilistic models (TPMs) such as hidden Markov models, which allow efficient computation of the probability that a generated sequence satisfies a formal grammar. It extends the GeLaTo framework, which previously used TPMs for lexical constraints, to hierarchical syntactic constraints described by LR(k) grammars. The key insight is that satisfaction of a finite-duration LR(k) grammar can be computed in polynomial time using dynamic programming over parse states.

Sentence-level hallucination detection revealed that while hallucination frequency differed little by source ideology, the content of hallucinations exhibited a robust leftward drift: a majority of hallucinated sentences were classified as left-leaning, even when the source article was right-leaning. Logit analysis showed that hallucinations occurred in high-entropy generation contexts, and in some models, token-level uncertainty predicted this leftward drift.
💡 Why It Matters： As LLMs are increasingly used to answer political questions, especially in election settings, systematic ideological drift in hallucinations could misinform users by consistently skewing information in one direction. The findings highlight urgent need for bias auditing and safeguards in high-stakes deployments.
📊 Key Facts: 21,727

Document-grounded QA requires LLMs to answer questions based solely on a provided document, but they sometimes generate unsupported statements called hallucinations. Ideological drift occurs when model outputs convey a political leaning not present in the source material. The QBias dataset provides a benchmark of news articles annotated for political bias, enabling measurement of such drift. Token-level logit analysis examines the model's confidence at each generation step, with high entropy indicating uncertainty that can lead to guessing.
Key Details

Hallucinations were detected by comparing model-generated answers against the source article at the sentence level. A fine-tuned stance classifier was used to label the ideological valence of hallucinated content. Across 8,127 replay episodes involving 10 models and 3 financial tasks, they found that frontier models agree on decisions 95% of the time while following the same tool path only 77% of the time—an 18-percentage-point gap (95% CI: [0.14, 0.22]). The study identifies three behavioral profiles: pattern matchers that collapse to a single output, stable executors with consistent tool use, and trajectory divergers that reach the same conclusions through different tool paths.
💡 Why It Matters： In high-stakes domains like finance, outcome-only evaluation misses process instability, threatening auditability and reliability. This benchmark provides a way to measure process consistency, challenging the adequacy of outcome metrics alone and encouraging more robust evaluation.

Beam search is a heuristic search algorithm that explores a limited set of the most promising candidates during code generation.
