LG AI Research Releases EXAONE Finance, Attention-Free Financial Forecasting Model

rss ArXiv cs.AI · 2026-09-07 · ⌛ 5 min read
Reason：Presents a novel attention-free financial time series foundation model with linear-time operators, addressing key limitations of existing TSFMs for finance. Technical depth is high and relevance to AI in financial markets is strong, though it is an arXiv preprint without community validation.
📰 What's New
LG AI Research introduced EXAONE Forecast for Finance, a financial time-series foundation model that replaces self-attention with causal 1D convolution for temporal mixing and group-aware pooling MLP for variate mixing. The model is pretrained on a corpus spanning equities, foreign exchange, commodities, crypto-assets, fixed income, and macroeconomic indicators. On the FinVerse benchmark, EXAONE Finance ranks first across all three evaluation tiers: point-forecast accuracy, cross-sectional asset ranking, and portfolio profitability. A masked context augmentation trains the model on contiguous missing spans to handle intermittently observed financial panels.
💡 Why It Matters： Financial institutions often face long, many-channel, intermittently observed panels where transformer-based models incur quadratic costs and assume fully observed inputs. EXAONE Finance's linear-time operators make it feasible to forecast across diverse asset classes without building separate models per market, potentially reducing infrastructure and modeling overhead for quant teams, asset managers, and hedge funds. State-of-the-art performance on FinVerse suggests zero-shot financial forecasting may become practical for portfolio construction and risk management.
Background
Time series foundation models (TSFMs) are large models pretrained on broad time-series data to perform zero-shot forecasting on new datasets. Most existing TSFMs use self-attention, whose memory and compute scale quadratically with sequence length and channel count, making them costly for high-frequency financial panels. Financial data often arrives with missing observations due to market closures, illiquidity, or asynchronous reporting. EXAONE Finance is an attention-free alternative that uses convolution and pooling MLPs to achieve linear scaling.
Key Details
The architecture uses two linear-time operators: causal 1D convolution for temporal mixing and group-aware pooling MLP for variate mixing, avoiding quadratic self-attention. Masked context augmentation exposes the model to contiguous missing spans during training, increasing robustness to real-world missingness. The pretraining corpus covers six asset classes, and evaluation on FinVerse includes point-forecast accuracy, cross-sectional asset ranking, and portfolio profitability.

Iris-mini and Iris-pro Set Top Open-Source Search Agent Results

rss ArXiv cs.AI · 2026-09-07 · ⌛ 6 min read
Reason：Presents a novel data pipeline and training recipe for large-scale search agents (35B/397B) with RL against live search; technically deep and relevant to AI practitioners, though no community discussion provided.
📰 What's New
Iris-mini (35B-A3B) and Iris-pro (397B-A17B) are search agents trained via a reverse-constructed multi-hop data pipeline and reinforcement learning against live search. The data pipeline builds entity graphs from the out-links of seed pages, rewrites non-answer entities into descriptive references so no clue can be resolved by string matching, and admits only questions that a reference model fails closed-book but solves with supporting evidence. RL training runs the reward judge and observation summarizer inside the training cluster, interrupting over-long rollouts at the request level and resuming from their committed prefixes. With inference-time context management enabled, the two models score 82.2/84.8/86.9/52.3 and 88.6/85.1/92.9/56.4 on BrowseComp, BrowseComp-ZH, DeepSearchQA, and HLE.
💡 Why It Matters： Open-source search agents have previously lagged proprietary systems; Iris's top results in their parameter ranges and planned release of weights plus the full data, training, and evaluation recipe could lower the barrier for building competitive search agents. The finding that inference-time context management affects benchmark scores more than most reported differences between systems encourages practitioners to evaluate both with and without it. The 397B-A17B mixture-of-experts design achieves high performance with relatively few active parameters, making it valuable for research groups with limited compute.
📊 Key Facts: 82.2 84.8 86.9
Background
Search agents use web search tools to gather evidence before answering; ReAct is an agent loop that interleaves reasoning and actions. SFT is supervised fine-tuning on demonstration trajectories, while RL optimizes a policy through rewards from live interactions. The notation 35B-A3B means a mixture-of-experts model with 35 billion total parameters but only 3 billion active per token, reducing inference cost. Multi-hop questions require chaining facts across multiple pages to answer.
Key Details
The training recipe alternates SFT and RL in a procedure called SFT-RL climbing, returning the hardest solved and most efficient rollouts of each RL round to the next supervised pass. Data construction only accepts questions a reference model fails closed-book yet solves once supporting evidence is supplied, and trajectories are filtered at both trajectory and turn levels before SFT. All results come from a single ReAct agent with no sub-agents and no test-time verification; evaluation fixes the tool set, context limit, and judge, and runs both with and without inference-time context management.

Better LLMs create correlated financial market behavior and raise systemic risk.

rss ArXiv cs.AI · 2026-09-07 · ⌛ 5 min read
Reason：High-value research paper by notable authors (incl. Andrew Lo) providing a framework and evidence that correlated behavior among frontier LLM agents can create non-diversifiable systemic risk in financial markets, with direct implications for AI deployment and financial stability.
📰 What's New
Researchers led by Jillian Ross and four co-authors report in arXiv:2609.04373 that more capable LLM agents exhibit significantly correlated behavior in financial market simulations. They develop a general framework showing how this correlation creates a non-diversifiable risk floor. Three findings emerge: frontier LLMs show correlated behavior that increases with capability; when their shared reasoning is accurate, increasing agent participation reduces market-level risk; and when agents share a common misinformation environment, the same correlated behavior becomes a liability. The researchers call this a capability paradox: improving individual models does not necessarily produce better system-level outcomes.
💡 Why It Matters： Banks and asset managers using similar frontier LLM trading agents could see correlated trading decisions amplify market swings or crashes. Because this correlation creates a risk floor that cannot be diversified away, even individually accurate models can generate systemic risk that financial regulators must monitor. The findings also suggest similar dynamics could affect other LLM deployment areas such as content moderation and hiring.
Background
LLM agents are autonomous systems that use large language models to make decisions, increasingly deployed in trading, content moderation, and hiring. Diversification normally reduces risk when individual errors are independent, but if many agents share training data or reasoning patterns, their errors can align, leaving a risk floor that cannot be diversified away. Systemic risk refers to the possibility that correlated failures cascade across a market or financial system.
Key Details
The authors decompose agent actions into shared and idiosyncratic components; when the shared component dominates, a non-diversifiable risk floor emerges. They use an agent-based simulation with LLM traders of varying general-purpose capability to compare behavior correlations. Results show correlation increases with capability, and shared misinformation environments turn the correlation from a stabilizing factor into a liability.

Reason：Presents a multi-agent system for automated TPU kernel generation with three distinct paradigms and evaluation on 50 kernels, representing significant progress in applying LLMs to hardware-level optimization. No comments provided to assess discussion quality.
📰 What's New
MaxKernel is a multi-agent system that implements three distinct paradigms for TPU kernel development: a Human-in-the-Loop (HITL) agent for collaborative step-by-step design, an Autonomous (Auto) agent for fully automated metric/trace-driven optimization, and a Graph-Based Autonomous Search that scales the Auto agent for global design space exploration. A shared pool of specialized sub-agents handles planning, implementation, self-debugging, testing, and hardware profiling. Evaluated on JaxBench's 50 diverse kernel tasks for TPUs and real-world workloads from open-source models, MaxKernel matches expert hand-tuned baselines. On eight production workloads, generated kernels outperformed human-written code by 2.02×.
💡 Why It Matters： Automating TPU kernel generation lowers the barrier to hardware-level optimization, enabling ML engineers without deep TPU expertise to obtain high-performance kernels. The open-sourced multi-agent framework could accelerate deployment of large models on Google TPUs by reducing manual tuning time and cost. Its success on 50 JaxBench kernels and production workloads suggests LLM-driven compiler feedback loops may become a standard practice for accelerator optimization.
📊 Key Facts: 2.02
Background
TPU (Tensor Processing Unit) is Google's custom AI accelerator, and a kernel is a low-level function that executes operations on the accelerator. Writing high-performance kernels requires deep knowledge of TPU memory hierarchy, data layout, and instruction set. LLM agents can use compiler feedback and hardware profiling to iteratively refine kernel code, replacing manual tuning. MaxKernel applies this agentic approach to TPU kernels, combining human guidance, autonomous optimization, and graph-based search.
Key Details
The system uses a shared pool of specialized sub-agents for planning, implementation, self-debugging, testing, and hardware profiling, enabling modular decomposition of complex kernel tasks. The HITL agent supports collaborative step-by-step design, while the Auto agent runs a fully automated metric/trace-driven loop and the Graph-Based Autonomous Search performs global exploration. Evaluation on JaxBench covers 50 kernel tasks, and in production tests MaxKernel generated code outperformed human-written implementations by 2.02× on eight workloads.

Sparse 0.05% Token Supervision Matches Full-Token LLM Reasoning Training

rss ArXiv cs.AI · 2026-09-07 · ⌛ 4 min read
Reason：Novel research finding that extremely sparse supervision (as few as 0.05% of tokens) can match or exceed full-token training for reasoning, with potential to significantly reduce training costs; no comments provided to assess community discussion.
📰 What's New
arXiv paper 2609.04565 reports that on-policy distillation with supervision on only one or two tokens per reasoning trajectory—about 0.05% of all generated tokens—matches or surpasses full-token training in improving reasoning for Qwen3 models. The finding holds across nine teacher–student configurations at different scales on mathematical reasoning tasks, and is further validated on coding reasoning, Llama models, and PPO-based RLVR. Sparse supervision may be closer to natural trial-and-error learning, updating only a few critical reasoning steps.
💡 Why It Matters： This undermines the assumption that effective post-training must be token-intensive. It suggests LLM reasoning can be improved with vastly less supervision data and compute, potentially cutting post-training costs for AI labs and enabling faster iteration on reasoning models.
📊 Key Facts: 0.05%
Background
On-policy distillation trains a student model on outputs generated by the student itself under a teacher’s guidance; typical implementations compute loss on every generated token. RLVR uses verifiable rewards (e.g., passing tests or checking answers) to reinforce reasoning steps. The new result contrasts with common post-training practices that devote massive compute to token-level updates.
Key Details
The setup uses on-policy distillation, where a teacher provides dense token-level supervision for every generated token; sparse supervision retains only one or two tokens per trajectory in the training objective. The tested family is Qwen3, with nine teacher–student configurations across model scales. The effect also generalizes to coding tasks, Llama models, and Proximal Policy Optimization with verifiable reward (RLVR).

📰 What's New
The yen carry trade is unwinding as monetary policy paths of major central banks diverge. Investors who borrowed yen at low interest rates to fund higher-yielding positions are now closing these trades. This unwind is causing shifts across global currency pairs and spilling over into risk markets such as equities and high-yield bonds. The move reflects a widening policy gap between the Bank of Japan's gradual normalization and continued easing or hold by other major central banks.
💡 Why It Matters： The unwind could tighten global liquidity by reducing the supply of cheap yen funding that has supported risk assets for years. Market participants from Japanese retail investors to emerging-market borrowers may face higher funding costs and increased volatility. If policy divergence persists, the re-pricing of carry trades may have prolonged effects on asset prices and currency correlations.
Background
A carry trade is a strategy that profits from interest rate differentials between currencies. Monetary policy divergence occurs when central banks set different paths for interest rates or balance sheet policies. In the case of the yen, the Bank of Japan's shift away from ultra-loose policy reduces the profitability of borrowing yen to invest elsewhere, prompting investors to exit and causing rapid currency and asset price movements.
Key Details
The carry trade involves borrowing in a low-yielding funding currency, typically the yen, and investing in higher-yielding currencies or risk assets to earn the interest rate differential. When the Bank of Japan tightens policy while other central banks hold or cut rates, the differential narrows and the trade becomes less attractive. Unwinding typically takes the form of short covering in the yen, which can amplify yen appreciation and force liquidations in crowded risk positions.
