Edge0 Serves 35B MoE from SSD at 20 tok/s on 24GB

rss ArXiv cs.AI · 2026-09-17 · ⌛ 4 min read
Reason：High-value technical innovation: Edge0 enables serving a 35B MoE model on consumer hardware (24GB, 3GiB active memory) at 20 tok/s using trained routing prediction and recovery LoRA, significantly reducing deployment cost barriers. No comments provided, but the novel prerouter approach and strong performance metrics are directly relevant to AI practitioners and investors focused on edge inference and cost efficiency.
📰 What's New
Researchers introduced Edge0, a streaming MoE inference engine that predicts next-layer routing one token ahead using a per-layer prerouter head. The prediction is consumed as the actual routing, so the staged expert set equals the routed set and no experts are dropped. On a single 24GB machine, Edge0 serves a 35B MoE at 20 tok/s within 3GiB of peak active memory, maintaining accuracy within a few points of its fp16 teacher across five public benchmarks. The framework, checkpoints, and adapters are open source, and an 8B tier runs on the same framework.
💡 Why It Matters： By cutting active memory for a 35B MoE from 19.5GB at 4-bit to 3GiB, Edge0 makes large MoE models deployable on consumer GPUs, laptops, and edge devices without cloud costs. This lowers the hardware barrier for developers and enterprises running high-quality sparse models locally, potentially shifting inference from data centers to edge environments.
Background
Mixture-of-experts (MoE) models activate only a subset of expert sub-networks per token, reducing compute but not weight storage, because all experts must be available in memory. Offloading to SSD is difficult because the next layer's experts depend on the current layer's output, creating a latency bottleneck known as the memory wall. Edge0's prerouter addresses this by predicting the next layer's routing before the current layer finishes, allowing SSD reads to overlap with computation.
Key Details
Edge0 uses a per-layer prerouter head that predicts the next layer's routing one token ahead; the prediction replaces the original routing decision, so only the predicted experts are staged from SSD. An unmerged recovery LoRA trained on the student path recovers quality lost to int4 quantization and routing replacement. The 35B model is 19.5GB at 4-bit, but Edge0 keeps only 3GiB active by streaming expert weights from SSD on demand.

Declarative configuration lets users describe the desired data schema instead of writing step-by-step generation logic, making pipelines easier to inspect, share, and reproduce.

SAFE Benchmark: Do Frontier Models Seek Safety Evidence Before Acting?

rss ArXiv cs.AI · 2026-09-17 · ⌛ 5 min read
Reason：The paper introduces a novel benchmark (SAFE) and provides systematic analysis of frontier models' safety evidence acquisition, revealing distinct behavioral policies across major models. This has high relevance for AI safety and deployment decisions, though it is an incremental research contribution rather than a paradigm-changing breakthrough. No community discussion is available.
📰 What's New
Researchers introduced SAFE, a controlled benchmark evaluating whether frontier models proactively acquire safety-relevant evidence before making deployment decisions. Across GPT-5.5, o3, Claude Opus 4.8, and Claude Sonnet 4.6, SAFE found distinct evidence-acquisition policies: Opus inspects nearly by default, o3 is the most skip-heavy and threshold-sensitive, while GPT-5.5 and Sonnet occupy intermediate regimes. Inspection increased strongly with severity and decreased with retrieval cost, but changing stated probability from 10% to 70% altered inspection by at most 21 percentage points. Stage 1 rationales were dominated by expected-value reasoning, and counterfactual interventions showed evidence framing changed decisions near the inspection boundary without being mentioned.
💡 Why It Matters： The findings shift evaluation from how models respond to known risks to whether they obtain evidence needed to know an action is safe, with direct implications for high-stakes deployment. o3's skip-heavy policy suggests it may proceed without checking even when evidence is available at low cost, while Opus's near-default inspection implies higher overhead but potentially better safety guarantees. These behavioral differences can inform model selection and monitoring for AI systems making autonomous decisions.
📊 Key Facts: 10% 70% 5.5
Background
Frontier models are the most advanced large language models, and deployment decisions involve automated actions that may carry safety risks. Existing evaluations typically assume safety information is already in context, whereas SAFE examines an earlier decision point: whether models choose to acquire safety evidence before acting. Expected-value reasoning is a decision strategy that weighs outcome probabilities and payoffs, and the study found it dominated the models' first-stage explanations.
Key Details
SAFE is a controlled benchmark where models make deployment decisions with optional evidence varying in retrieval cost, probability, severity, and presentation. The cost-obligation decomposition found that avoidance is driven primarily by retrieval friction and explicit threats to the deployment payoff, rather than by remediation duties created by knowing. Counterfactual interventions showed that evidence framing strongly changes decisions near the inspection boundary but is largely unmentioned in model rationales, while probability is frequently cited despite little causal influence.

OBC-Prune Uses Outcome-Based Calibration to Improve Large Reasoning Model Pruning

rss ArXiv cs.AI · 2026-09-17 · ⌛ 5 min read
Reason：Novel pruning method for large reasoning models addresses a critical efficiency issue, but is an incremental research advance rather than a paradigm shift; no community discussion provided to gauge engagement.
📰 What's New
OBC-Prune, an outcome-based calibration method for large reasoning model pruning, constructs difficulty-matched pairs of correct and incorrect rollouts from problems the model answers inconsistently. Intervention-based analysis estimates the causal importance of each reasoning sentence by measuring how removing its influence affects subsequent predictions. These causal importance scores are converted into per-token weights that rescale calibration activations for one-shot pruning methods including SparseGPT, Wanda, and ALPS. Experiments on DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen 1.5B, 7B, and 14B models at 40% and 50% sparsity show consistent improvements on MATH500, LiveCodeBench, and AIME 2025.
💡 Why It Matters： Large reasoning models generate long chain-of-thought traces, increasing inference cost; pruning can reduce that cost but often preserves erroneous reasoning patterns. By prioritizing weights behind correct reasoning, OBC-Prune reduces both error propagation and trace length, making deployment of 1.5B–14B reasoning models cheaper without architectural changes. This matters for inference providers and edge deployments where latency and token counts directly determine cost.
📊 Key Facts: 40% 50%
Background
Large reasoning models (LRMs) produce long chain-of-thought traces before answering, which increases inference latency and cost. Pruning removes less important weights to reduce compute, but calibration data determines which weights are considered important. Recent calibration uses the model's own rollouts, but treats all reasoning tokens uniformly, failing to distinguish correct from erroneous reasoning.
Key Details
OBC-Prune uses difficulty-matched pairs generated from inconsistent answers, then estimates causal importance by removing a reasoning sentence's influence and measuring changes in subsequent predictions. The causal scores are converted into per-token weights that rescale calibration activations for SparseGPT, Wanda, and ALPS without modifying the pruning algorithms. The approach targets 40% and 50% sparsity, and improves over state-of-the-art calibration baselines on MATH500, LiveCodeBench, and AIME 2025 across most model sizes.
