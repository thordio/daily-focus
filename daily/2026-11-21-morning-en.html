📰 What's New
Thinking Machines Lab released Inkling 2 Mini on November 20, 2026 under Apache 2.0. The open-weight model uses a modified looped transformer architecture where layers 6-17 are looped three times, activating only 5.4B parameters while accepting text and image inputs and generating text. It supports up to 1 million tokens of context with adjustable reasoning effort. Early local inference tests show 27 tokens/s decode on a Ryzen 395 with llama.cpp Vulkan and about 10 tokens/s on an M1 Pro MacBook using q4 xl. Benchmark results place it at the level of Qwen3.8-27B.
💡 Why It Matters： Because Inkling 2 Mini matches far larger models while running on consumer hardware, it could shift open LLM development toward looped architectures. Developers and enterprises can deploy a multimodal coding agent locally without cloud GPUs, reducing inference costs and privacy risks. If open-weight models continue to match models several times their size, LLM deployment economics change for edge and privacy-sensitive use cases.
📊 Key Facts: 2.0 3.8
Background
Most transformer decoders execute each layer once per token. Looped transformers reuse a block of layers multiple times, reducing parameter count and storage while adding compute depth through repeated forward passes. Inkling 2 Mini loops layers 6-17 three times, yielding much lower disk and memory footprint than a model with equivalent effective depth. Qwen3.8-27B is a 27B-parameter model from Alibaba's Qwen series used as a performance reference. Apache 2.0 permits free use, modification, and distribution.
Key Details
Inkling 2 Mini's looped transformer runs layers 6-17 three times; combined with embeddings, only 5.4B parameters are activated, and training is done end-to-end in nvfp4 precision. It accepts text and images, supports up to 1 million tokens of context, and offers adjustable reasoning effort. A patch for llama.cpp (for unmerged PRs 32694, 32657, 32429, 32704) yields about 30% speed boost.
