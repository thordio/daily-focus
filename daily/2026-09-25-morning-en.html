Privileged Self-Practice Improves Multi-Turn Agent Training Beyond Self-Distillation

rss ArXiv cs.AI · 2026-09-25 · ⌛ 4 min read
Reason：Proposes a novel training method (Privileged Self-Practice) that addresses a critical failure mode in on-policy self-distillation for multi-turn LLM agents, with empirical evidence showing base model outperforming OPSD-trained agents. High technical relevance for RLHF/GRPO practitioners; no community discussion available.
📰 What's New
Researchers propose Privileged Self-Practice (PSP), which moves privileged information from the loss function to the sampling process. When a student model's rollouts on a task mostly fail, an analyzer model writes a short per-task instruction; the task is resampled with that instruction in context, and training proceeds with an unchanged GRPO objective. Across AppWorld and SWE-bench Verified with three different student models, PSP achieved the best average score in every setting and improved task-goal completion by up to 65% on AppWorld and resolved rate by up to 61% on SWE-bench Verified. The paper also shows that conventional on-policy self-distillation (OPSD) can yield performance below even the untrained base model.
💡 Why It Matters： For practitioners using GRPO or RLHF to post-train multi-turn LLM agents, PSP offers a simple fix that avoids training agents to act confident without underlying information. The reported gains of up to 65% on AppWorld and 61% on SWE-bench Verified suggest this approach could replace self-distillation in agent post-training pipelines.
📊 Key Facts: 65% 61%
Background
On-policy self-distillation (OPSD) post-trains a model by token-level supervision from a stronger teacher view of itself, created by conditioning on privileged information (PI). GRPO (Group Relative Policy Optimization) is a reinforcement learning objective commonly used for LLM training. Multi-turn agents execute tasks over several steps, where privileged information may include hidden environment state or oracle feedback unavailable at inference.
Key Details
PSP leaves the GRPO objective unchanged and injects privileged information only as prompt context during resampling, never in the loss. An analyzer model generates a short per-task instruction when rollouts mostly fail. The method was evaluated on AppWorld and SWE-bench Verified with three different student models.

ReliabilityRoute Dynamically Routes Forecasting Agent Behaviors by Reliability Features

rss ArXiv cs.AI · 2026-09-25 · ⌛ 6 min read
Reason：Important technical progress in AI forecasting agents, introducing a structural intervention (ReliabilityRoute) that selects among reasoning, retrieval, market priors, and analogs based on reliability features. Relevant to AI practitioners building forecasting systems and could influence agent architecture decisions. No comments provided to assess community discussion.
📰 What's New
Researchers introduce ReliabilityRoute, a structural intervention that routes forecasting agent behaviors — reasoning, retrieval, market-prior use, and historical analogs — based on six reliability features: historical coverage, market-prior availability, source-prior sharpness, evidence strength, evidence disagreement, and horizon. On ForecastBench-style binary forecasting tasks, mechanism choice was source-dependent: structured analogs dominated for some data-generating processes, while market/crowd-style and conservative baselines were better for others. A fixed 2024-fitted rule matched a hand taxonomy without hard-coded source-name decisions, and a walk-forward self-adjusting rule refitted thresholds from previously resolved vintages. The self-adjusting rule achieved the best mean Brier score among deterministic systems across 16 later LLM vintages, though the gain was modest and historical/search baselines remained highly competitive.
💡 Why It Matters： For AI practitioners building forecasting systems, the result suggests that adding more reasoning is not automatically better; agents should first estimate which evidence source deserves control before generating a forecast. The reliability-based routing method provides a reproducible path to improve trustworthiness under auditable constraints, and its modest gains caution teams that historical/search baselines remain competitive.
Background
Forecasting agents are AI systems that combine language-model reasoning, retrieval, ensembling, and calibration to predict future events. Binary forecasting tasks ask for probabilistic yes/no predictions and are commonly scored with the Brier score, which penalizes squared error between predicted probabilities and actual outcomes. A market prior treats crowd or betting-market probabilities as a baseline, while historical analogs match the current question to similar past events. Reliability routing means dynamically selecting which internal behavior should produce the final forecast for each question.
Key Details
ReliabilityRoute treats the choice to retrieve, reason, defer to a market prior, or use a historical analog as an observable agent behavior rather than a hidden implementation detail. Its reliability features include historical coverage, market-prior availability, source-prior sharpness, evidence strength, evidence disagreement, and horizon. The walk-forward self-adjusting version refits thresholds from previously resolved vintages and achieves the best mean Brier score among deterministic systems across 16 later LLM vintages.

Pistis Introduces 27B and 9B Multimodal Models with Interleaved Distillation and RL

rss ArXiv cs.AI · 2026-09-25 · ⌛ 6 min read
Reason：Introduces a new multimodal LLM family with a novel post-training method (IDRL) that could influence AI training practices; technical depth and novelty are notable, but no direct market/economic impact and not a breakthrough architecture.
📰 What's New
The Pistis model family introduces 27B- and 9B-parameter multimodal large language models built on Qwen3.6 and Qwen3.5 respectively. A new post-training paradigm called Interleaved Distillation and Reinforcement Learning (IDRL) alternates on-policy distillation and reinforcement learning within a single training loop, instead of using a static joint loss. The framework first establishes a foundation through large-scale multimodal supervised fine-tuning (SFT), then produces two specialized variants at both scales: Pistis-Thinking for deep multimodal reasoning and Pistis-Agentic for long-horizon planning, iterative reasoning, and tool use. Pistis-Agentic is particularly strong in multimodal search. The team also introduces Pistis-Auto-Harnessing (PAH), a system-level method that iteratively optimizes the agent's inference harness without updating model parameters or increasing the interaction budget.
💡 Why It Matters： IDRL's alternating optimization offers a practical alternative to static multi-objective post-training, potentially reducing capability trade-offs when a single model must handle both reasoning and long-horizon agentic tasks. Developers building multimodal AI agents for search, planning, and tool use gain two targeted variants instead of a single general model, while PAH can improve deployed agent performance without retraining or higher API interaction costs. The methods may influence how open-weight multimodal models are post-trained at the 9B and 27B scale.
📊 Key Facts: 3.6 3.5
Background
Qwen3.5 and Qwen3.6 are releases in Alibaba's Qwen family of open-weight large language models; multimodal versions also process images and text. Supervised fine-tuning adapts a pretrained model on labeled examples, while reinforcement learning optimizes behavior through rewards. On-policy distillation transfers knowledge from a teacher model using data generated by the current policy, and agentic trajectories are multi-step sequences of observations, actions, and tool calls. An inference harness is the external scaffolding that manages tool calls, parsing, and control flow around a model.
Key Details
The 27B model builds on Qwen3.6 and the 9B model on Qwen3.5. IDRL tightly integrates on-policy distillation and reinforcement learning by alternating objectives, which the authors report improves knowledge transfer, optimization stability, and credit assignment for long-horizon agentic trajectories. PAH operates at the inference harness level and improves performance without model parameter updates or additional interaction budget.
