It provides actionable insights for autonomous procurement deployment and highlights necessary guardrails, making it highly relevant for AI practitioners and investors in agentic systems.
📰 What's New
A large-scale study tested nine LLMs from OpenAI, Google, and Alibaba in 9,840 agent-to-agent supply chain negotiations. Agents reached agreement in 98.9% of cases and captured 95.4% of the optimal undiscounted surplus. Baseline models accepted loss-making contracts in 19.2% of cases, compared to 0.0-0.6% for more capable models. The identity of the AI provider markedly influenced surplus division: self-play buyer shares were 40% for OpenAI, 50% for Google, and 70% for Alibaba's Qwen.
💡 Why It Matters： Companies deploying autonomous procurement agents need automated profit verification to prevent weaker models from agreeing to money-losing deals. The delay-induced surplus erosion highlights the need to optimize negotiation speed. Vendor choice is not just a technical decision but a strategic one that directly impacts bargaining power in supply chain negotiations.
📊 Key Facts: 98.9% 95.4% -34%
Background
Supply chain bargaining often involves asymmetric information: the buyer knows demand while the seller does not. Perfect Bayesian Equilibrium is a game-theoretic solution concept for such settings. LLM agents are increasingly being explored for autonomous procurement, but their behavior in strategic interactions like bargaining has not been systematically evaluated until now.
Key Details
The study used a canonical supply chain bargaining model with a privately informed buyer. A Perfect Bayesian Equilibrium served as the benchmark. The agents' 'strategic patience'—separated from the principal's economic patience via prompting—was the strongest driver of surplus division, explaining 90% of outcome variance.

rss ArXiv cs.AI · 2026-08-11 · ⌛ 4 min read
Reason：Introduces a structural solution to hypothesis drift in automated research loops, incorporating falsifiability and user taste, with potential to improve reliability and direction in AI-driven scientific discovery.
📰 What's New
Researchers built an AI Scientist system with immutable experiment cards, specialized subagents, and a preference oracle called kkanbu. They ran the identical loop twice across eleven research streams, with and without kkanbu. Neither arm drifted: both falsified about three-quarters of their own hypotheses, and the best trained policy came from the oracle-less arm. The oracle changed research direction: it alone explored test-time adaptation, authored the winning designs where its arm led, and carried lessons across streams that the other arm repeatedly re-derived.
💡 Why It Matters： This structurally addresses metric drift in automated research, where LLM-driven loops often optimize for a metric rather than testing motivating hypotheses. By enforcing falsifiability and allowing user taste to guide exploration, it makes AI scientists more reliable and directed, with potential impact on fields like robotics and drug discovery that rely on large-scale experimentation.
Background
In automated research loops, LLM-driven experiments often drift to optimize a specific metric, forgetting the original hypothesis. This hypothesis drift undermines scientific falsifiability. Quadruped navigation is a challenging domain requiring learned generalization policies. The work builds on Karpathy's autoresearch paradigm, adding structural rigor to maintain hypothesis testing.
Key Details
The system uses an immutable experiment card with a fixed schema to pair each iteration's prediction with its outcome, preventing retrospective hypothesis adjustment. Specialized subagents are restricted to mechanical roles, and kkanbu, a typed knowledge graph holding the user's research taste, is the sole component permitted to make subjective judgments. The eleven research streams focused on generalization of quadruped robot navigation policies in simulation.

Mendel Gödel Machine: Recursive Self-Improving Coding Agents via Comparative Evolution

rss ArXiv cs.AI · 2026-08-11 · ⌛ 4 min read
Reason：Proposes a novel self-improving AI paradigm by incorporating comparative evolution from Mendelian genetics, potentially overcoming limitations of single-trajectory methods; includes theoretical proof and simulations.
📰 What's New
Researchers introduced the Mendel Gödel Machine (MGM), a recursive self-improving coding agent that adds two new self-modification strategies inspired by Mendelian inheritance: reaction-norm mutation and cross-lineage hybridization. Unlike prior single-trajectory methods, MGM leverages the agent's archive of past attempts to perform edits based on multiple tasks and reference lineages. The paper includes theoretical proof under an additive fitness landscape model and demonstrates faster convergence in surrogate simulations. Experiments on SWE-bench and Polyglot benchmarks show consistent improvements in performance, efficiency, and generalizability.
💡 Why It Matters： MGM addresses the limitation of single-trajectory self-improvement by incorporating comparative signals, leading to more efficient and robust coding agents. This could accelerate progress in automated software engineering, where AI agents autonomously fix and generate code. The approach also contributes to the broader field of self-improving AI, with potential safety implications as agents become better at recursively modifying their own code.
Background
A traditional Gödel machine (proposed by Jürgen Schmidhuber in 2003) is a theoretical self-referential program that can rewrite its own code once it proves the new version is optimal. MGM extends this by introducing a comparative evolution mechanism, treating an agent's past task attempts as a genetic archive, enabling beneficial code edits derived from across different tasks and agent lineages.
Key Details
The two novel mutations are: reaction-norm mutation, which edits based on an agent's trajectories on multiple tasks; and cross-lineage hybridization, which uses a reference agent from another lineage on the same task. Theoretical analysis uses an additive fitness landscape to prove convergence advantages. Experiments cover the SWE-bench verified split and 12 languages in Polyglot, with results indicating improved task success rates and code reuse efficiency.

📰 What's New

The method operates solely on weight space, requiring no input data.
💡 Why It Matters： As open-weight LLMs are developed through complex multi-stage pipelines, determining model origin, ownership, and evolution is critical for governance, supply-chain integrity, and IP protection. Background
LLM development often involves fine-tuning, merging, and continued pre-training of base models, creating intricate lineage relationships. Traditional provenance methods might require training data or specific architectures. This weight-space approach is analogous to biometrics, using intrinsic weight properties like SVD-based fingerprints, which have been explored in recent works such as GhostSpec, but the current framework unifies and extends these ideas for robust lineage tracing.
Key Details
The method analyzes weight matrices from two perspectives: spectral energy captured by singular value distributions for global magnitude patterns, and subspace alignment quantified by subspace deviations for directional geometry. Spectral energy handles coarse-grained separation, while subspace alignment achieves fine-grained discrimination among shared-base models, distinguishing nuances like dataset proportions and post-training adjustments.
