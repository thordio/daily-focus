Ovis-Embedding Introduces Universal Omni-Modal Embedding Family

rss ArXiv cs.AI · 2026-09-23 · ⌛ 4 min read
Reason：Introduces a state-of-the-art omni-modal embedding model with native shared backbone and training innovations, offering significant technical depth for multimodal AI; no comments provided to evaluate discussion quality.
📰 What's New
Ovis-Embedding introduces a family of omni-modal embeddings that natively integrate text, image, video, and audio through a shared multimodal backbone, using a pretrained Qwen-omni model adapted via contrastive training with low-rank initialization. Training incorporates homogeneous-source sampling for task-consistent batches and focal loss to emphasize hard examples, plus similarity-based Embedding Distillation from complementary experts. The family achieves state-of-the-art performance on MMEB-v3, MMEB-v2, MVEB, MAEB, and RTEB benchmarks.
💡 Why It Matters： A single unified model for text, image, video, and audio can replace multiple modality-specific encoders, reducing deployment complexity and operational costs for cross-modal search, recommendation, and retrieval-augmented generation systems. Developers and enterprises handling mixed media gain a simpler stack and potentially faster inference without maintaining separate modality towers.
Background
Omni-modal embeddings map text, image, video, and audio into a common vector space for any-to-any retrieval. Traditional approaches often used separate encoders per modality with additional alignment modules, increasing parameters and training complexity. A shared backbone natively encodes all modalities, potentially reducing parameters and improving cross-modal consistency.
Key Details
At inference, low-rank feature decomposition produces compact embeddings with flexible dimensionality and minimal performance loss. Similarity-based Embedding Distillation transfers fine-grained similarity structure from complementary expert models. Homogeneous-source sampling forms task-consistent batches with informative in-batch negatives to improve data efficiency.

Skill Habit Formation Makes AI Agents Consistent for Repeat Tasks

rss ArXiv cs.AI · 2026-09-23 · ⌛ 4 min read
Reason：The paper identifies significant inconsistency (38-74% disagreement) and waste (95-97% redundant planning) in AI agents and proposes a novel skill habit formation method. This is a high-value technical contribution for practitioners building reliable agent systems, though no community discussion is available to gauge validation.
📰 What's New
Travis Weber and colleagues ran 42 tasks three times each and found that 38% to 74% of answers disagreed depending on the model. They also measured that 95.3% to 97.2% of agent generation goes to re-deriving plans the system already knows. The proposed skill habit formation method lets an agent mine its own execution history for deterministic skill variants that declare input regions, so common cases run as scripts and the rest fall through to reasoning. On text-to-SQL, a habit-formed variant reproduced its output on all 456 repeated dispatches, used 14% to 56% fewer tokens, and turned net positive after 7 to 53 reuses.
💡 Why It Matters： Buyers, auditors, and regulators require repeatable outputs, but current agents are inconsistent on repeated work. By substituting deterministic skills for repeated planning, the method creates an auditable execution path and reduces inference cost, which matters for enterprises deploying agents on recurring workflows. The text-to-SQL results show a practical path to non-inferior accuracy with substantial token savings.
📊 Key Facts: 38% 74% 95.3%
Background
AI agents typically use large language models to plan and execute multi-step tasks, but repeated runs can produce different outputs because the model re-plans from scratch. Text-to-SQL converts natural language questions into SQL queries. Execution history records an agent's past runs and can be mined for reusable deterministic skills.
Key Details
Candidates pass through four gates of ascending cost; the central gate tests a candidate's execution trace against a retained reference within a tolerance derived from that reference's own run-to-run variability. A guard admitted work it should have deferred on 2.6% of natural paraphrases and 26% of inputs near its boundary, and 11 of 13 such failures were invisible to the trace-conformance gate at any threshold. Separating routing from parameter extraction raised end-to-end accuracy from 0.888 to 0.952 at 43% of cost.

rss ArXiv cs.AI · 2026-09-23 · ⌛ 5 min read
Reason：Novel technical research combining dynamic Gaussian splatting with joint-embedding predictive architecture for temporal composition; potentially advances 3D scene forecasting but is a preprint without empirical validation or market relevance. No discussion comments provided.
📰 What's New
A new architecture called 4DGS-JEPA introduces a Gaussian-native joint-embedding predictive model for causal multi-horizon prediction over dynamic Gaussian scenes. It uses a hierarchical scene-, motion-group-, and Gaussian-level representation together with a horizon-conditioned transition operator that supports both direct prediction and recursive rollout. Endpoint and multi-horizon path supervision anchor predictions to future target embeddings, while a selective geometry decoder and geometry-level composition ground the learned dynamics in consistent group motion and Gaussian geometry without requiring complete future appearance reconstruction. Three controlled experiments provide evidence that temporal composition reduces latent path dependence while retaining predictive accuracy.
💡 Why It Matters： Existing dynamic Gaussian Splatting methods are optimized mainly for reconstruction, future-state generation, or rendering, not for learning reusable predictive dynamics. 4DGS-JEPA's temporal composition principle addresses latent path dependence, and its hybrid correspondence mechanism combines persistent canonical identity with residual optimal-transport matching to stay robust under reordering and topology change. This could enable more efficient multi-horizon simulation and planning in dynamic 3D scenes where future appearance is uncertain.
Background
Dynamic Gaussian Splatting represents evolving 3D scenes explicitly with Gaussian primitives, but most methods focus on reconstruction or rendering rather than predictive dynamics. Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPA) learn by predicting representations in latent space instead of reconstructing raw pixels. Temporal composition requires that different chronological paths leading to the same future endpoint produce compatible predictive states, which helps reduce path dependence in sequential prediction.
Key Details
The architecture includes a hierarchical representation at scene, motion-group, and Gaussian levels, and a horizon-conditioned transition operator with direct and recursive rollout modes. It uses endpoint and multi-horizon path supervision to align predictions with future target embeddings. The hybrid correspondence mechanism combines persistent canonical identity with residual optimal-transport matching, and the work characterizes zero-loss path agreement and finite-error rollout accumulation theoretically.

Attention as a Routing Graph Extracts Circuits in One Forward Pass

rss ArXiv cs.AI · 2026-09-23 · ⌛ 5 min read
Reason：Presents a novel interpretability method that extracts circuits from a single forward pass, with causal validation on known tasks and significant efficiency gains. Relevant to AI researchers, but scope is relatively narrow and no community discussion is provided.
📰 What's New
A new method extracts language model circuits by treating attention as a routing graph from a single forward pass, then keeps a small set of routes that point toward the answer. On induction and IOI tasks across GPT-2 Small, GPT-2 Medium, and Pythia-410M with n=100 prompts per cell, ablating extracted edges hurts the model much more than ablating a random set of the same size. The extraction step costs one forward pass, while a head-by-head patch sweep costs about two orders of magnitude more. Code and evaluation artifacts are published at https://github.com/Aquinf03/live-circuit-routing.
💡 Why It Matters： Mechanistic interpretability circuit discovery currently relies on numerous causal interventions, making it expensive for larger models. A single-forward-pass circuit sketch could reduce compute by roughly 100x for initial circuit hypotheses, allowing researchers to screen more models and tasks before committing to costly validation. This may broaden circuit analysis beyond GPT-2-scale models toward larger systems where intervention sweeps are prohibitive.
Background
Circuit extraction in language models typically identifies subgraphs of attention heads and MLP neurons responsible for specific behaviors, often through activation patching or ablation across many forward and backward passes. Induction tasks test whether a model can repeat patterns like 'AB...AB', while IOI (Indirect Object Identification) tests whether it can identify an indirect object in a sentence. GPT-2 Small and Medium are transformer language models from OpenAI with 124M and 355M parameters respectively; Pythia-410M is an open model from EleutherAI with 410M parameters. Mechanistic interpretability uses such circuits to understand how models compute outputs.
Key Details
The method keeps only attention routes that point toward the answer, then validates them by ablation against random edge sets of the same size. Evaluation uses paired gap tests and bootstrap confidence intervals with n=100 prompts per cell on GPT-2 Small, GPT-2 Medium, and Pythia-410M. The paper does not claim a complete circuit atlas but a cheap sketch with real causal signal and documented failure modes.
