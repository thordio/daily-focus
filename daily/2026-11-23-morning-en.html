GLM-5.4 Runs 145B Parameters on Laptop at Over 80 Tokens/s

rss 量子位 · 2026-11-23 · ⌛ 4 min read
Reason：Significant technical achievement: running a 145B parameter model locally on a laptop at 80+ tokens/s with zero token cost and offline capability, which could influence edge AI development and reduce inference costs. No comments available to gauge community discussion.
📰 What's New
Zhipu's GLM-5.4, a 145-billion-parameter large language model, has been tested running locally on a laptop at over 80 tokens per second. The model operates with zero token cost and can run offline, enabling local Vibe Coding without network access. Zhipu stated that GLM-5.4 validates computational depth and that a significantly larger next model will carry forward the learnings.
💡 Why It Matters： Running a 145B model on commodity laptop hardware removes the memory bandwidth bottleneck that has kept large models in the cloud, potentially cutting per-token inference costs to zero for developers. This could accelerate edge AI adoption for privacy-sensitive and offline coding tools, and pressure cloud inference providers as local deployment becomes practical. Zhipu's transparency around architecture also signals a shift toward more open technical disclosure in frontier LLM development.
📊 Key Facts: 5.4
Background
Traditional Transformer models compute each layer once per token, moving each layer's weights from high-bandwidth memory to the processor, so memory bandwidth often limits inference speed. A looped Transformer reuses one set of weights across multiple computation steps, reducing data movement and allowing larger models to run on memory-constrained devices. This is relevant for on-device deployment where cloud connectivity and per-token costs are unavailable.
Key Details
The technical approach uses a looped Transformer architecture, where one set of layer weights is computed repeatedly instead of unrolling distinct weights per layer, reducing HBM-to-chip data movement during token generation. This targets the memory bandwidth bottleneck that has dominated LLM inference. The implementation achieves over 80 tokens per second on a laptop with zero token cost and offline operation.

New Theory Shows Self-Similar Predictive Information Structure in Language

rss ArXiv cs.AI · 2026-11-23 · ⌛ 4 min read
Reason：Presents a novel theoretical framework for predictive information in language, with empirical validation via scaling collapse; significant for understanding LLM learning, though still a preprint without peer review.
📰 What's New
The arXiv preprint 2611.26935v1 develops a theory of how predictive information is distributed across preceding text, based on the scaling behavior of a family of information measures. The theory predicts that if this organization is self-similar across all measured length scales, information measures at different lengths collapse onto a common form. The authors observe this scaling collapse in long text documents, showing predictive information exists at every measured length scale with a self-similar structure. Self-similarity constrains the text-generating process and is stronger than merely showing long-range dependencies.
💡 Why It Matters： This matters because LLMs trained via next-token prediction must exploit predictive information in text; the self-similar organization means conditioning on more distant text continues to reveal new structure. It provides quantitative support for training LLMs with longer contexts and suggests a theoretical basis for how language structure becomes available to models across scales.
Background
Predictive information is the amount of information in a text segment that reduces uncertainty about future text, typically quantified via mutual information. Long-range dependence only indicates statistical correlations between distant tokens, while self-similarity requires that such correlations repeat in the same form at all scales. Scaling collapse is a method from physics used to detect critical phenomena, where rescaling data from different scales makes them coincide, revealing scale invariance.
Key Details
The theory uses a family of information measures, including predictive information (mutual information between future and past text), and examines its scaling behavior with history length. Scaling collapse means data from different lengths can be rescaled to lie on the same function, indicating scale invariance. The study validates this empirically on long text corpora without relying on a specific model architecture, measuring directly from text statistics.

Decision Models Serve as Zero-Shot Process Reward Models for RL Agents

rss ArXiv cs.AI · 2026-11-23 · ⌛ 5 min read
Reason：Introduces a novel zero-shot method to turn general-purpose decision models into process reward models, achieving significant gains on ALFWorld and WebShop without step labels or fine-tuning. High technical novelty and potential to simplify RL for language agents, though not yet validated at large scale or peer-reviewed.
📰 What's New
Researchers introduced DM-PRM, which converts a general-purpose decision model's probabilistic yes/no answers into step-level process rewards without step labels, fine-tuning, or a critic. Using Qwen2.5-1.5B, DM-PRM lifts GRPO by over twenty points on ALFWorld and over ten points on WebShop. It overtakes GiGPO on ALFWorld and learns in far fewer iterations, while also improving GiGPO and GraphGPO on both benchmarks. The approach asks one standardized yes/no question per step and adds the standardized answer to the advantage, plugging into any policy-gradient method.
💡 Why It Matters： This zero-shot conversion eliminates the need for expensive step labels, learned critics, or LLM judges that currently bottleneck language-agent RL. Agent developers working on household, web shopping, and other interactive tasks can now obtain step-level credit with a single decision model and one question template per environment, simplifying pipelines and reducing compute costs. Because it improves GRPO, GiGPO, and GraphGPO, it may accelerate adoption of policy-gradient RL for language agents across research and applied settings.
📊 Key Facts: 2.5
Background
Process reward models (PRMs) provide step-level credit during RL, in contrast to outcome rewards that assign the same signal to every step in an episode. General-purpose decision models answer yes/no questions with calibrated probabilities, originally used for decision-making rather than reward assignment. Policy-gradient RL methods like GRPO (Group Relative Policy Optimization) and GiGPO update language agents based on reward signals; without step-level credit, all steps in successful or failed rollouts are credited equally. ALFWorld and WebShop are text-based interactive benchmarks for household tasks and web shopping, respectively.
Key Details
DM-PRM asks one yes/no question per step and uses the decision model's probabilistic answer, standardized, as an additive term in the advantage calculation. It supports GRPO, GiGPO, and GraphGPO, and works with a single question text per environment across household and web shopping tasks. The method's step scores can locate useful steps within failed rollouts, and trained agents drop idle actions and explore instead of circling.

📊 Key Facts: 4.5 2.0
Background
Supervised fine-tuning (SFT) optimizes a model to produce the correct tool call but does not compare it against plausible incorrect calls. Memorization and Generalization in Generative Models: An Energy-Based Associative Memory View

rss ArXiv cs.AI · 2026-11-23 · ⌛ 5 min read
Reason：Provides a novel theoretical perspective linking diffusion models to associative memory, offering potential insights into generalization and creativity in generative AI. High conceptual depth and relevance for AI research, though no community discussion is available to assess validation.
📰 What's New
The thesis shows that diffusion models behave as associative memories: with little data, each training sample forms a distinct basin of attraction; as data grows, these basins shrink and spurious states emerge as attractors that blend stored samples but match none. The transition is characterized by measuring volume, energy, and curvature of basins across datasets and architectures including Stable Diffusion. For language, uniform-state discrete diffusion models are shown to be associative memories whose basins arise from conditional likelihood maximization; as the training set grows, basins around training examples contract while basins around unseen examples expand, until corrupted test sentences are recovered as reliably as training ones. Conditional entropy exposes this coexistence within a single sequence and cheaply diagnoses where a model lies along the transition.
💡 Why It Matters： This framework offers a concrete criterion—conditional entropy—for diagnosing whether a generative model is over-memorizing or generalizing, which matters for evaluating and auditing large models like Stable Diffusion. By reframing spurious states as creative rather than defective, it may shift how researchers design training objectives and interpret model outputs. The unification of memorization and generalization as one retrieval process challenges the common assumption that they are opposed.
Background
Associative memory stores patterns as energy minima and retrieves them via attractor dynamics; spurious states are stable attractors that do not correspond to any stored pattern. Energy-based models define probability distributions through an energy function; diffusion models can be viewed as learning to reverse a noise process. This thesis connects image diffusion models like Stable Diffusion and discrete diffusion language models under a shared attractor-basin framework.
Key Details
Attractor basins are quantified by volume, energy, and curvature; spurious states are energy minima corresponding to blends of stored patterns. Uniform-state discrete diffusion language models form basins of attraction purely from conditional likelihood maximization, without an explicit energy function. Conditional entropy measures the coexistence of memorization and generalization within a single sequence and serves as a cheap diagnostic.

AstronautBench Measures LLM Agent Self-Improvement via Knowledge, Evidence, and Progress

rss ArXiv cs.AI · 2026-11-23 · ⌛ 6 min read
Reason：Introduces a novel benchmark for evaluating LLM agent self-improvement across knowledge, evidence, and progress, addressing a gap in existing evaluation methods. No community discussion signals provided.
📰 What's New
Researchers introduced AstronautBench, a benchmark built on the game Outer Wilds that measures LLM agent self-improvement through three views: knowledge, evidence, and progress. Each episode is a 22-minute loop after which the world resets, and only the agent's knowledge carries over. After every episode, the benchmark measures places reached, ship-log rumor facts revealed, correctness of answers to 164 questions about the world, and whether each answer is supported by the frames and positions the agent cites from its own recorded play. Two LLM judges from different model families check every answer. In eight-episode baselines with GPT-6.1 Sol and Gemini 4 Argon planners, the three views do not move together; the GPT-6.1 Sol agent's correct answers grew from 14.6% to 26.8% of all questions, but at episode 8 only 59 to 68% of them were supported by its cited evidence, and it revealed no new rumor facts after episode 3.
💡 Why It Matters： Existing benchmarks typically measure task progress, world-knowledge QA, or episodic memory separately, so they cannot show whether an agent actually observed the evidence behind a correct answer. AstronautBench reveals that knowledge growth and evidence support can diverge: progress and memory may keep increasing while newly correct answers cite only earlier evidence. This matters for training and evaluating embodied LLM agents, as relying on a single metric can overstate an agent's ability to learn from experience.
📊 Key Facts: 14.6% 26.8% 68%
Background
Outer Wilds is an exploration game in which the player uncovers the history of an alien civilization within a 22-minute time loop; after each loop the world resets, so only knowledge carries over rather than physical items. In LLM agent evaluation, 'self-improvement' refers to an agent improving across episodes using its own experience with fixed model weights, not through retraining. The ship log in the game tracks 'rumor facts' that the player discovers, and many areas can only be reached after acquiring the corresponding knowledge.
Key Details
The benchmark uses Outer Wilds, where each episode is a 22-minute time loop after which the world resets; only the agent's knowledge carries over, and progress is gated by that knowledge. After every episode, it evaluates 164 world-knowledge questions, places reached, ship-log rumor facts revealed, and whether each answer is supported by the frames and positions the agent cites from its own recorded play. Two LLM judges from different model families check every answer; the eight-episode baselines include GPT-6.1 Sol and Gemini 4 Argon planners acting through a learned controller or their own gamepad inputs.
