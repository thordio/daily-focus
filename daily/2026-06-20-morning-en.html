ITNet: Learnable Integral Transform Unifies Convolution, Attention, Recurrence

rss ArXiv cs.AI · 2026-06-20 · ⌛ 5 min read
Reason：Proposes a unified mathematical framework (learnable integral transform) that subsumes convolution, attention, and recurrence, potentially representing a paradigm shift in neural architecture design.
📰 What's New
Researchers Ashim Dhor, Rasel Mondal, and Pin Yu Chen introduced the Integral Transform Network (ITNet), a neural architecture built on a learnable integral transform. ITNet uses an MLP to implement a kernel that subsumes convolution, multi-head self-attention, and autoregressive recurrence—including LSTM, GRU, S4, and Mamba—as special cases. Three techniques—tiled kernel fusion, importance-weighted Monte Carlo integration, and learned low-rank factorization—enable efficient, scalable computation. A single ITNet matches or exceeds specialized baselines on ImageNet-1K, GLUE, ModelNet40, VQA v2, and NLVR2.
💡 Why It Matters： Unifying convolution, attention, and recurrence into one learnable operator challenges the assumption that distinct tasks require fundamentally different architectures. This could streamline model development across vision, language, and other domains, reducing reliance on hand-crafted inductive biases. ITNet’s ability to match specialized models suggests that a single adaptive mechanism can learn the right inductive bias from data, paving the way for more general and scalable AI systems.
Background
Convolutional networks apply a fixed local pattern across all positions, suitable for images; recurrent networks process sequences step-by-step with a hidden state; transformers use self-attention to compare all pairs of elements. These three families have historically been mathematically distinct, leading to fragmented research. An integral transform generalizes them by expressing the output as a weighted combination of input values, with a learnable kernel that can encode locality, memory, or content-based interactions based on data.
Key Details
The kernel is an MLP that jointly processes position and feature embeddings, enabling adaptive interaction type selection. ITNet is proven to be a universal approximator of continuous operators. Tiled kernel fusion reduces memory overhead, importance-weighted Monte Carlo integration enables efficient sampling, and learned low-rank factorization approximates the full kernel with reduced dimensions, making the architecture practical for large-scale tasks.

ICT Framework Uses Token Distribution Deviations to Stabilize RL for LLM Reasoning

rss ArXiv cs.AI · 2026-06-20 · ⌛ 4 min read
Reason：The paper introduces a novel framework (ICT) that addresses a fundamental optimization instability in RLVR for LLM reasoning, shifting from scalar entropy to distributional properties of token logits. This is a significant technical contribution with potential to improve training stability and reasoning quality, though it lacks immediate market impact or community validation.
📰 What's New
The Independent Combinatorial Tokens (ICT) framework identifies tokens with distinctive distributional patterns via Jensen-Shannon divergence between token logit distributions, addressing entropy collapse and explosion in reinforcement learning for reasoning. On Qwen2.5 models (0.5B/1.5B/7B), updating only the top 10% of unique tokens yields an average pass@4 improvement of 4.58% over GRPO, 20-Entropy, and STAPO baselines, with a maximum gain of 14.9% across seven benchmarks covering math and Olympiad problems. Theoretical analysis grounded in Shannon and second-order Rényi entropy proves that this selective updating reduces overall Shannon entropy while controlling probability concentration measured by second-order Rényi entropy.
💡 Why It Matters： Reinforcement learning for LLM reasoning often suffers from instability due to uniform token updates causing either premature convergence or chaotic exploration. ICT offers a precise mechanism to stabilize training by focusing on a small fraction of critical tokens, drastically improving reasoning performance with less computation. This approach can reshape RLVR practices for complex reasoning tasks.
📊 Key Facts: 10% 4.58% 14.9%
Background
In Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) for LLM reasoning, uniform policy updates on all tokens can cause entropy collapse (overly deterministic outputs) or entropy explosion (incoherent exploration). The ICT framework mitigates this by identifying and selectively updating tokens with abnormal distributional patterns, maintaining an effective exploration-exploitation balance.
Key Details
ICT uses Jensen-Shannon divergence to measure distributional deviation of token logits, shifting optimization from scalar entropy to distributional properties. The dual effect proven theoretically: it reduces overall Shannon entropy while controlling probability concentration via second-order Rényi entropy. Only 10% of unique tokens need updating to achieve substantial gains across diverse reasoning benchmarks.

💡 Why It Matters： This work addresses two critical barriers to AI adoption in regulated finance: the need for auditable decision trails and the requirement that client data remain on-premises. 📊 Key Facts: 71.24% 63.56% 68.2%

In regulated finance, any AI system must produce auditable records of its reasoning to satisfy auditors and compliance officers, and many firms require that client data never leave their own servers. Existing chart QA agents typically focus on raw accuracy using black-box models that lack transparency and rely on external APIs. A multi-agent pipeline decomposes the task into smaller, specialized steps, allowing each to be optimized and audited independently.

Tested on interactive environments ALFWorld and WebArena, where trajectories are long and procedurally rich, MATM improved downstream task performance and reduced interaction steps without requiring coordination or joint training.

Key Details
MATM extends retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) from human-authored artifacts to agent-generated trajectories, storing them in a shared repository for retrieval by consumer agents. It operates without explicit inter-agent coordination, making it suitable for long, procedurally structured trajectories in environments like ALFWorld and WebArena.

Hidden Anchors Model Explains LLM Deliberation Dynamics

rss ArXiv cs.AI · 2026-06-20 · ⌛ 5 min read
Reason：Presents a novel dynamical system model for multi-agent LLM deliberation with testable predictions, advancing understanding of how deliberation improves reasoning beyond initial beliefs, which is relevant for designing better AI collaboration systems.
📰 What's New
A closed-loop dynamical system model has been proposed for multi-agent LLM deliberation, where each agent possesses a hidden internal belief called an anchor that continuously pulls its opinion. Tested across three open-weight LLM families, the model shows that agents' confidence can surpass the initial belief range—a behavior classical consensus models cannot explain. A method to recover the hidden anchor solely from deliberation data was developed, along with a generalization test to validate the model's applicability.
💡 Why It Matters： Understanding the hidden anchor mechanism enables better design of multi-agent AI systems for complex reasoning tasks, potentially improving accuracy beyond individual model capabilities. The model explains how group deliberation can produce outcomes superior to any single agent's initial belief, which is crucial for developing robust ensemble methods. The framework also bridges AI collaboration and human opinion dynamics, offering insights into collective intelligence.
Background
Multi-agent LLM deliberation involves multiple language models exchanging and refining answers over several rounds to improve accuracy. Classical opinion dynamics models like DeGroot and Friedkin-Johnsen assume agents converge to a compromise within the range of initial beliefs without persistent individual biases. Hidden anchors represent an agent's intrinsic conviction that continuously influences its opinion, allowing the group to reach conclusions that surpass any single agent's initial viewpoint—a phenomenon mathematically described as escaping the convex hull of initial opinions.
Key Details
The model represents deliberation as a closed-loop dynamical system where each agent's opinion update is a weighted sum of its neighbors' opinions and its hidden anchor. The anchor can be recovered from deliberation data using linear regression on opinion trajectories. Testing across three open-weight LLM families revealed that anchors are equally influential, but only when the anchor lies far from initial opinions does the group's confidence escape the convex hull of starting beliefs.

Cross-Model Attribution Reveals LLM Epistemic Blind Spots on Clinical Data

rss ArXiv cs.AI · 2026-06-20 · ⌛ 4 min read
Reason：Novel investigation into LLM epistemic calibration on structured clinical data, revealing critical gaps in confidence estimation and inverse difficulty effects; directly relevant for AI safety and reliable deployment in high-stakes domains. No community discussion provided.
📰 What's New
A study comparing Qwen 2.5 7B and XGBoost on clinical tabular data prediction found that LLM verbalized confidence is nearly constant (0.856-0.937) regardless of accuracy, tracking prompt format rather than prediction quality. The model displays an inverse difficulty effect: accuracy drops to 64.8% when XGBoost is 99% confident and correct, yet matches XGBoost (73.8% vs 73.1%) when the latter is moderately uncertain. Few-shot examples combined with SHAP-derived feature evidence reduce the Attribution Disagreement Score from 1.54 to 0.38 and lift accuracy from 49% to 75.3% without any training. A cross-model calibrator using attribution divergence signals lowers expected calibration error from 0.254 to 0.080, replacing uninformative verbalized confidence with patient-specific reliability estimates.
💡 Why It Matters： This exposes a critical safety gap for LLM deployment in high-stakes domains like healthcare, as models cannot self-assess their own knowledge on structured data. The cross-model attribution approach offers a practical way to detect epistemic blind spots without internal access or repeated inference, charting a route toward genuine epistemic self-awareness in LLMs.
📊 Key Facts: 64.8% 99% 73.8%
Background
Epistemic uncertainty stems from the model’s lack of knowledge, distinct from aleatoric uncertainty due to data noise. XGBoost is a high-performance gradient-boosted tree algorithm widely used for tabular data, and SHAP (SHapley Additive exPlanations) assigns feature importance scores based on cooperative game theory. Attribution divergence measures disagreement between models’ explanations, signaling where one model may be unreliable.
Key Details
The experiments evaluate Qwen 2.5 7B against XGBoost via attribution divergence analysis on clinical tabular data. The few-shot and SHAP interventions are orthogonal and super-additive, meaning their combined effect exceeds the sum of individual effects. The calibrator operates solely on attribution signals, requiring no model internals or extra inference passes.
